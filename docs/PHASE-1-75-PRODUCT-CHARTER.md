> This is the project's fourth constitution. It does not change the festival's identity, the brand personality, the visual grammar, or the EVOKE Test. It changes one thing: **what the product is for**. Earlier documents framed the deliverable as "the official website"; this charter ratifies the corrected purpose — a registration and participant platform whose festival identity makes registration feel safe and exciting.

---

## 1. What this platform is

**EVOKE 2K26 is a premium event registration and participant experience platform for the college's annual inter-school fest.** Its purpose is to help participants confidently **discover events, understand requirements, register, complete payment, and stay informed** — all within a memorable, cohesive festival identity.

The brand and storytelling exist to make that experience **enjoyable and trustworthy** — not to overshadow it.

## 2. What it is NOT

- NOT the festival's general promotional site.
- NOT a storytelling microsite (the story serves the journey; it is not the product).
- NOT an exhibition built for admiring the festival.

**The identity is the vehicle; registration is the journey.**

## 3. The Participant Journey (primary user journey)

Someone hears about EVOKE → visits → understands what EVOKE is → explores events → reads rules → chooses an event → registers → pays → receives confirmation → returns for updates

1. **Arrival** — "What is EVOKE?"
2. **Orientation** — "Which event is right for me?"
3. **Eligibility** — "Can I participate?"
4. **Rules** — "What exactly is expected?"
5. **Decision** — "I choose this event."
6. **Registration** — "How do I register?"
7. **Payment** — "Paying is safe and clear."
8. **Confirmation** — "I am registered."
9. **What's next** — "I know what happens now."

## 4. Product philosophy (replaces "every event deserves its own spotlight")

> **Every participant should feel confident before they register.** Registration should feel effortless, informed, and trustworthy. Remove uncertainty from every step of participating in EVOKE.

The product is **participant-centric**, not event-centric. Events keep their full information and personality — but success is measured by **confidence**, not admiration. No event is an afterthought; none is given a louder stage than its information deserves.

## 5. Progressive Confidence (design principle)

At every step, the user must be **more certain than they were before**:

- Homepage → "I understand what EVOKE is."
- Events → "I found the event I want."
- Event page → "I know exactly what's expected."
- Registration → "I know what information I need."
- After payment → "I'm successfully registered."

If a user reaches payment with doubts like "Am I eligible?" or "Did I miss a rule?", the earlier pages failed.

## 6. The decision filter (4 questions — every future design decision)

Does this decision:

1. Help someone choose an event?
2. Reduce uncertainty before registration?
3. Improve trust during registration and payment?
4. Reinforce the EVOKE identity?

**If any answer is no, the decision is removed or redesigned.** The EVOKE Test (Phase 1.5, Part 9) catches the generic; this filter catches the off-purpose.
