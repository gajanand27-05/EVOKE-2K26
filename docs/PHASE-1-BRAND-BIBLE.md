## 1. Brand Personality

### 1.1 The central question

> *"If EVOKE '2K25 were a living personality, who would it be?"*

**EVOKE '2K25 is The Electric Ringmaster.**

A young, supremely capable conductor who steps into a spotlight, adjusts a self-made bright collar, and turns each person in the audience into a performer for sixty seconds. Not through noise — through a festival of *becoming*: a quiet pencil becomes a monument, a pile of waste becomes a treasure, a nervous child becomes the voice of the room.

Everything about EVOKE is **transformation** — Phase 0 Section 4 gives the evidence:

- Vegetable Carving: vegetables become cars, ships, animals (4.11)
- Face Painting: a face becomes a canvas (4.9)
- Cooking Without Fire: raw ingredients become platters (4.10)
- Best Out of Waste: trash becomes treasure (4.16)
- Solo Song: a nervous student becomes the voice of the room (4.5)
- G.K. Quiz: a written test becomes a stage for four teams (4.2)

The brand's DNA is the **before-and-after** — the moment something ordinary becomes extraordinary. The Ringmaster is competitor, host, cheerleader, and friend who hands everyone a slip, a pot, a track, a script. He is a **producer of micro-visions.**

### 1.2 The brand as a person

- **Name:** The Electric Ringmaster (working identity)
- **Age:** mid-teens — old enough to command a stage, young enough to be one of the students. Around **16**, and the edition mark (2K25) matures with him but never ages him.
- **Voice:** warm, generous, precise. He announces with flourish ("And NOW, the stage lights up for…") but checks the clock like a pro. He does not yell; he is **loud with a cue, not loud with volume.** Humor is deft, never goofy; inclusive, never mocking.
- **Character:** confident but **never arrogant**, disciplined but **never rigid**. He obeys the rule book — because a festival that lies about its own rules loses its audience. He is the youngest person in the room running it like it matters.
- **Attitude:** "Catch the spark before you know what to do with it." Ready to applaud instantly. Enjoys the loud talkies, the silent concentration, the traded pens.
- **Values:** fairness (the omnipresent "Judges' decision is final" — Phase 0 3.3.1), originality (the anti-copying clauses — 3.3.3), wholesomeness (decency everywhere — 3.3.3), courage (the solo spots — 4.5), community (the big teams — 4.6), craft-care (the quiet hours — 4.15).
- **Energy profile:** intermittent — peak, then cast. Not flat excitement; it is **energy that breathes**: a full roar, then a hush while an instrument is tuned. The visual system must pace like that (Section 9).

### 1.3 If EVOKE were…

| If EVOKE '2K25 were… | it would be |
|---|---|
| **a person** | The Electric Ringmaster. |
| **a movie** | A school-set story where the shy and the loud share one stage and both win — the film of the students, not the winners; a soundtrack of the crowd more than the chorus. |
| **a song** | One melodic, upbeat indie-electronic track that bubbles up like friendship for 3–4 minutes, then ends on one long, earned note (Phase 0 4.4–4.7). |
| **a place** | The pre-show yard of a theatre at dusk — equal parts backstage and workshop: a gold spotlight corner, a pot under a lamp, a quiet sketching bench, chatter near the entry. |
| **an art style** | Indian-Modernist papercraft — bold vernacular patterns matched with heritage motifs and an ink wash; hand-made but precise; nothing that feels like a repeated template. |

### 1.4 What the personality is NOT

- NOT the "tech-loud, we-are-Gen-Z" neon hypersurface (Phase 0 5.2.1: one idea executed with restraint).
- NOT a "king" ego-drama — the competitive energy is communal, not sovereign (4.6 "the tribe").
- NOT a corporate event brand (2.2 — coordinators demand logistics and calm, not hype).
- NOT a one-trick celebration machine. It "brings out the best in YOU" — the personality exercises humility: it raises the participant up, never the brand.

### 1.5 Why this is right, not just trendy

- **Traceable.** Every pillar fingerprints back to Phase 0 (rule passages 3.3, event personalities 4.x, tagline 1.1).
- **Enduring.** An archetype, not a trend — it can survive 2K26, 2K27 without a redesign (Phase 0 5.2.8, 1.7: annual institution).
- **Solves the Phase 0 audience tension (2.9).** The Ringmaster is the rare identity that is *at once* magnetic/fun for Students and structured/clear for Coordinators — emotion first, information second, both held by one personality.
- **Differentiating.** It escapes the school-website graveyard of gradient blues (5.2.2, principles 11–12). A controlled spark, held in check.

### 1.6 Voice & tone guidelines

| Scenario | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Announcing an event | **Performance flourish** | "Fashion: the whole runway is yours. 60–90 seconds. Lights." |
| Explaining a rule | **The Rule Keeper** — clear, gracious, neutral | "Per the rule book, the judges' decision is final." |
| Reminder/deadline | **The Stage Manager** — short, warm, punctual | "Registrations close at 6:00 PM. Don't leave it to the last bell." |
| Parent / school query | **Calm assurance** — simple words, no hype | "Every event has a written rule. Every decision is final and recorded." |
| Celebrating a win | **Generous applause** — names the student, not the brand | "That round belonged to them." |

*This table is the seed for Phase 3 (content/voice). Fixed in principle; open in execution.*

## 2. Emotional Design — the emotional journey

### 2.1 The planned emotional arc

Emotion is an **arc over time**, not a flat maximum:

| Time | Visitor should feel | What delivers it |
|---|---|---|
| **0–3 s** | "Whoa — this is not a normal school site." Immediate kinship. | A color-field breath + one distinctive departure (Section 4). The first beat of the Ringmaster. |
| **3–10 s** | Curiosity + a grin. "Okay this is not boring. I can live here." | Recognizable identity; a flicker of motion that matches the grammar (Section 9); one clear "apply" affordance. |
| **10–30 s** | Self-placement. "Which one is ME?" (dance, art, quiz?) — and for a coordinator, "where are the rules?" | Events presented as personalities, not as a form (6.5 mini-experience). |
| **1–2 min** | "This is real. I can see myself hero." | Social proof (5.2.9) + the "stage you can picture yourself on" (5.4). |
| **after leaving** | FOMO + identification. "I could be on that stage." | The site told a story rather than giving an info dump. |

### 2.2 Peaks and valleys

- **Peak 1 — The Instant Reveal (0–3s).** The stage opens. One powerful visual statement.
- **Valley 1 — The Hush.** After the hero: a quiet, logical "here is what happens next." This valley is *by design*: trust after joy (2.3 parents need calm).
- **Peak 2 — The Event Spark.** Flickering across a specific dream (dance, quiz, sketch) — small rising swells backed by event personality (4.x).
- **Peak 3 — The Invitation.** The registration moment is the applause beginning — the tide turns from watching to joining (7.1 journey end).

### 2.3 Intentional valleys (calm is designed)

- **Logistics Calm:** rules, eligibility, schedule, contact pages are literally calmer — more negative space, neutral surfaces, smaller type. Not a failure; a Phase 0 decision (2.2: coordinators need "accurate, logistical calm").
- **No constant screaming.** An everything-shouts homepage wears out both audiences; the loud is rationed like applause.

### 2.4 What should remain the next day

- **The sentence:** "Bring out the best in You."
- **The feeling:** "I could be that, on a stage."
- **A concrete anchor:** the date, the venue, and ONE event that belongs exactly to them.
- **A frame:** the distinctive color-and-type language — recognizable at a glance even with the logo removed (the project goal).

## 3. Visual Direction — three strong but honest concepts

### 3.1 Direction A — "Backstage Glow" *(cinematic, dramatic, lit)*

- **Narrative:** The site is the interior of a darkened hall the moment before the curtain lifts. Deep ink and charcoal; gold (the spotlight), rose (the warm glow), spare white beams. Roskilde's "lights in the night" energy (Phase 0 5.2) with a Whiplash tension.
- **Mood:** theatrical, intense, premium.
- **Strengths:** photography-first; reads "stage" instantly; intensity admits the "wholesome but gripping" tension (no filler — the festival's stage is real).
- **Weaknesses:** Parents (2.3) want calm reassurance — dark UI risks neglecting them; heavy media risks performance (principle 8); fragile without gallery assets (Q14); could read as a TV-event rather than an inter-school one.
- **Risk / Longevity:** high-maintenance, photo-dependent; classic if executed superlatively, plays badly if not.
- **Originality:** strong on the stage side; common across music-festival land.

### 3.2 Direction B — "The Craft Floor" *(material, handmade, warm)*

- **Narrative:** The site is the face-paint / pot-paint / sketch floor (4.9–4.15). Warm paper surfaces, clay-toned bursts, thread-like outlines, low motion. The crafting hands are visible — the "animal carved from a carrot" wonder.
- **Mood:** warm, honest, tactile.
- **Strengths:** authentic and approachable — parents trust it (2.3); photography-light and performance-safe; never corporate (5.2 premium-by-honesty).
- **Weaknesses:** under-sells the stage glamour (4.3 Fashion Show, 4.6 Group Dance) — the festival's loud heart; risks feeling like a school craft sale.
- **Risk / Longevity:** ages gracefully but may never inspire a student to sign up for the stage.
- **Originality:** fresh for school-site land, but only half the festival's soul.

### 3.3 Direction C — "The Standing Poster" *(graphic, editorial, brand-first)*

- **Narrative:** The site is one enormous moving poster: bold typographic headlines, flat graphic shapes, a proprietary pattern language, confetti-like accents. An identity that holds even with zero photography. Think the graphic confidence of the best festival brands (SXSW's graphic identity, Lowlands' typography — 5.2) without copying any.
- **Mood:** confident, youthful, crafted, loud-but-organized.
- **Strengths:** performance-light; the brand survives photo-less seasons (Q14 pending); the pattern language can carry clusters and events (6.5); it scales to print, social, posters — the "logo-removed identity-stays" goal; the strongest backbone for a multi-year system (5.2.8).
- **Weaknesses:** risks flatness or "poster-cold" if warmth and motion are not added; needs a strong typographic point of view or it reads as a template.
- **Risk / Longevity:** the safest long-term asset: controllable, reusable, upgradable.
- **Originality:** high when paired with a distinctive pattern vocabulary rather than generic confetti.

### 3.4 Critique and recommendation

**Honest critique:**

- Direction A has the emotion I want, but it is **fragile**: photographic-heavy, performance-risky, parent-risky (Phase 0 Q14 gallery unknown, principle 8).
- Direction B has the humanity, but it **under-sells the festival's loud heart** (4.6, 4.3).
- Direction C is the strongest **backbone**, but on its own can feel flat; it needs A's energy and B's warmth injected deliberately.

**Recommendation — "the Stage-Crafted Hybrid":**

Adopt **Direction C as the global brand skin** (graphic, typographic, pattern-driven identity), performed **on a surface range**:

- **Identity moments** (home, heroes, announcements): Direction C at full voice — bold, graphic, warm-dark or warm-paper depending on context.
- **On-stage events & moments** (Stage/Mind clusters): Direction C + the light-language of A — spotlit surfaces, gold/rose accents, deep-ink sections.
- **Off-stage events & moments** (Art/Maker clusters): Direction C + the material language of B — paper, clay, thread, visible craft.
- **Logistics surfaces** (rules, schedule, registration): the calm, neutral, typographically clean C-skin with strong hierarchy.

This is the only direction that, stripped of every photo, still looks exactly like EVOKE — satisfying the primary objective and both Phase 0 principle 13 (age well) and principle 8 (performance). The composite also honors principle 4 (no repeated layouts): event pages differ by cluster skin while sharing one identity.

## 4. Color Philosophy

### 4.1 What emotions color must create (from Phase 0 §1.5)

| Emotion | Color role |
|---|---|
| **Anticipation / excitement** | The **announce** gesture — warm-on-dark: amber/gold against deep ink |
| **Pride / belonging** | A strong, confident **identity ink** — deep warm ink that carries the whole system |
| **Curiosity** | Color as wayfinding: a distinct treatment per cluster (4.17) |
| **Clarity / reassurance** (2.2, 2.3) | Cooling neutral fields; functional colors, never decorative |
| **Joy / playfulness** | A single high-key **spark accent** — playful without rainbow chaos |
| **Aspiration** | The "open stage" metaphor — deep ink interrupted by warm light |

### 4.2 What should never happen

- **Gradient festivals** (fuchsia→cyan) — the AI-magnet, template look.
- **Orange/teal "creative tech"** — too close to every startup site.
- **Sixteen rainbow event colors** — noise that breaks coordinator scanning (2.2).
- **All-pastel pages** — "baby school."
- **Default saturated violet/magenta** — the generic "creative" default.
- **Gray-only pages** — reads as template. Neutrals are the *frame*, never the soul.

### 4.3 What is overused on school websites (and why we refuse it)

- **Navy → light blue → white** — reads authority/dashboard; kills excitement (Phase 0 §1.5 vs 5.2).
- **Rainbow borders/balloons** — the literal cliché of "school event."
- **Purple** — overused for "creativity" in exactly this domain.
- **Full white + single blue button** — the default template.
- **Stock-photo hero + white sections** — invisible identity.

### 4.4 The color philosophy — "Light on Stage, Craft in Warmth"

The system is built on **one duality**: the festival's stage (light) and its craft (warmth). Both live inside one restrained, deliberate palette.

| Role | Concept | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary identity** | **Ink** — a deep, warm near-black (charcoal with amber undertone) | The darkened-hall stage floor; anchors all light gestures; gives the identity weight and premium feel |
| **Secondary** | **Paper** — warm cream off-white | The craft surface; the calm of the workshop; the text-bearing surface on light sections |
| **Accent (announce)** | **Amber/Gold** — the spotlight | All "moment" gestures: CTAs, announcements, the stage-light metaphor, countdown |
| **Spark (joy)** | **Vermilion/Rose** — one warm red-pink | Playful accents: tags, stickers, celebration moments — the applause color |
| **Cool balance (mind)** | **Teal/Indigo-leaning cool** — used sparingly | G.K. Quiz/Mind cluster wayfinding; functional "info" affordances; contrast partner to warm accents |
| **Backgrounds** | Ink (dark sections) and Paper (light sections) | No random colors; every surface is one of the two, per cluster context (3.4) |
| **Surfaces** | Tints/shades of Ink & Paper + one subtle warm neutral | Cards, panels, inputs — always derived from the same family, never new hues |
| **Interactive** | Amber on Ink / Ink on Paper | One interactive color pair; hover shifts brightness, never hue |
| **Success** | A calm green (eco — Best Out of Waste 4.16) | Only for true confirmation states |
| **Warning** | Amber deepened toward ochre | Only for genuine warnings (deadlines) |
| **Danger** | A restrained red | Only for errors and disqualification-critical info |
| **Neutral** | Warm gray scale derived from Paper/Ink | Text, dividers, disabled states |
| **Text** | Ink-on-Paper / Paper-on-Ink, with a mid-gray for secondary text | Contrast-first: always AA (principle 7) |

**Cluster tints (not new colors):** each cluster (4.17) gets a *treatment* of the existing system — Stage = Amber/Rose on Ink; Mind = Teal on Ink; Art = Paper + graphite; Maker = Paper + clay-warm. Same palette, different proportions. Coordinator layouts (2.2) never see more than two hues at once.

**Why amber and not red or blue:** red reads alarm/wrong; blue is the school cliché. Amber is the color of the spotlight, of gold medals, of a warm evening — it carries stage energy AND warmth, serving both audiences (2.9). Vermilion gives play; teal gives intellect; neither fights amber because they sit opposite in hue without gradient slop.

## 5. Typography Philosophy

### 5.1 Typography is branding

Type is the festival's second voice. The Ringmaster announces in display type and explains in body type; the distinction matters as much as the characters themselves.

- **Personality:** the display voice is **confident and musical** — letters that feel like announcements; the body voice is **warm, open, human** — a friend explaining the rules.
- **Hierarchy:** three clear levels only — Display (announce), Heading (direct), Body (explain). If something cannot fit the three, it is probably decoration (principle 14).
- **Contrast:** the system lives on **scale contrast** — very large display vs. compact body — rather than weight-juggling. Contrast by size, not by shouting.
- **Rhythm:** typographic rhythm mirrors event rhythm (4.x): loud beats (display) and quiet beats (body) alternate deliberately.
- **Spacing:** generous line-height on body; tight but intentional display tracking; numerals are the festival clock — always tabular (time limits, dates, counts).
- **Readability:** body text stays readable at small sizes on mobile (principle 9); never below accessible minimums (principle 7).
- **Expression:** display type is allowed to *perform* — stacked, stretched, cropped — but only on identity moments (2.1), never in rules or registration.

### 5.2 Recommendations (families, not brands)

| Role | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Display family** | A high-contrast, characterful **display sans with musical rhythm** (a contemporary grotesque with distinct personality) | Announces like a Ringmaster; handles large scale, tight tracking, editorial stacking; stays legible at hero sizes on mobile |
| **Body family** | A **humanist sans** with open apertures, warm feel | Reads like a friendly explainer; performs on Paper and Ink; strong at small sizes for coordinators |
| **Display usage** | Reserved for identity moments, event names, the tagline, numbers | Rarity keeps it special (2.1 Peak 1; principle 10) |
| **Numerical typography** | **Tabular figures** everywhere numbers appear (dates, time limits, team sizes, counts) | Time is the festival's scarcest resource (3.3.5); rule tables must align perfectly (2.2) |
| **Button typography** | Body family, medium weight; uppercase only on identity buttons | Buttons are actions, not decoration — clear and calm |
| **Navigation typography** | Body family, small-medium, clear active state | Wayfinding must be instant for coordinators (2.2) |

**Type as story:** the tagline "Bring out the best in You" deserves a signature typesetting (stacked, generous, amber on ink) that repeats nowhere else — a visual signature like a stage light.

## 6. Iconography

- **Philosophy:** stroke-based, geometric with rounded joins — the **stage-light-and-craft-tool language**. Icons are wayfinding, not decoration (2.2).
- **Stroke vs filled:** a thin-medium consistent stroke for all functional icons; **filled, chunky forms only for the event marks** (identity moments).
- **Outlined vs rounded:** rounded outer corners, sharp inner details — a "handmade-precise" tension that matches the Ringmaster (1.2).
- **Geometric:** yes — icons derive from a small set of geometric parents (circle, square, triangle, line) so the system stays coherent and reducible.
- **Illustrative:** reserved for the event marks: each of the 16 events gets a **distinct mark** drawn from its transformation story (4.x) — a slip, a pot, a carved carrot, a half-painted face. Marks are the festival's memory hooks.
- **Minimal:** an icon is drawn with the fewest possible shapes; if it needs three colors, it is not an icon.
- **Communication:** icons carry *category* instantly (music, craft, food, mind) — a coordinator scanning 16 events reads them by shape before text (2.2).
- **Event icons as storytelling:** yes — the before/after DNA (1.1) lives in marks that imply transformation: a slip becoming a gesture, a pot becoming painted. The mark system is a Phase 2 deliverable to explore, not frozen here.

### 6.1 Traceability

§2.2 (scannability), 4.x (event personalities), 6.5 (event pages), 12 (principles 1, 10).

## 7. Shapes

- **Corner philosophy:** a **controlled radius scale** — large organic radii on identity/cards, small radii on logistics surfaces, zero radius on data/table elements. Corners are semantic: rounder = friendlier moment; sharper = operational clarity (2.2).
- **Geometry:** the system's parent shapes are **the circle (spotlight) and the square/rectangle (frame, slip, stage)**. Triangles appear only as direction/duration markers (time, arrows).
- **Circles:** stage-light metaphors — behind faces, above heroes, as counters; never decorative floats.
- **Squares:** the "slip" (Dumb Charades), the frame, the stage — rectangular frames crop all imagery (a consistent, recognizable crop language).
- **Organic forms:** allowed only inside Art/Maker surfaces (4.9–4.16) — painted, carved, pasted shapes — always bounded by the rectangular frame (discipline + play).
- **Layering & depth:** depth is achieved by **offset stacking** (a second copy of a shape shifted) — like paper layers, like a stack of slips; never heavy 3D.
- **Masks & cutouts:** imagery appears through cutouts — spotlight circles, torn-paper edges on Art/Maker, punched holes like confetti. Cutouts are the "before/after" reveal device.
- **Frames & borders:** a single signature frame language — a 1px warm outline on identity moments, none on logistics. Borders are *announcements*, used rarely.
- **Rhythm:** shape rhythm alternates organic and geometric with the page's energy (4.17): loud pages get more circles; calm pages get more straight lines.

## 8. Imagery

- **Photography style:** documentary-real — candid, alive, unposed. Students mid-laugh, hands mid-carve, a crowd mid-roar. Never stiff portraits, never stock smiles.
- **Cropping:** uniform rectangular frames (Section 7) with face/hand emphasis — faces for stage events, hands for craft events (4.x).
- **Composition:** off-center, energetic, with generous negative space for the frame and the pattern language to live.
- **Lighting:** stage events — warm spotlights, contrast, glow; craft events — soft natural window light; logistics — neutral, clean.
- **Color grading:** one global grade — warm, amber-leaning, film-like grain; consistent across all photography so the site reads as one film (principles 1, 10).
- **Illustration:** used at identity moments and for event marks; follows the pattern language (Indian-Modernist papercraft — 1.3) — flat, bold, hand-made; never inside logistics.
- **Textures & patterns:** a proprietary pattern vocabulary (Section 3.3): confetti dots, slip-edge lines, clay swatches, spotlight rays — reused as the "brand wallpaper" at low opacity.
- **Noise:** a subtle film grain over imagery (warm) and a subtle paper grain over light surfaces — texture = craft (4.x); never heavy.
- **Background philosophy:** surfaces are either Ink or Paper (4.4); imagery never floats on random backgrounds.
- **Overall feel:** **mixed, orchestrated** — photographic for stage, illustrative for identity, photographic-lite for craft, none for logistics. One film, three scenes.

## 9. Motion Language

- **If the brand moved:** it moves like the Ringmaster — **announcements are quick and confident; explanations are slow and steady; applause is instant.** Motion never runs on its own; it is cued (1.2).
- **Speed:** two speeds only: **fast (150–250 ms)** for reveals/transitions that feel like cues; **slow (500–700 ms)** for emotional pans (identity moments). Nothing in between, nothing arbitrary.
- **Character:** **elastic on celebration** (springy overshoot on stage moments, confetti) and **linear on logistics** (strict, mechanical, predictable). The motion character changes by scene, exactly like the color surface (3.4).
- **Confident vs playful:** confidence = content-in (fade + rise, once, no repeat); playful = micro-bounce on interactive elements only.
- **Purpose rule (principle 2):** every animation answers "what is it telling?" — a countdown ticks, a reveal reveals, a transition transitions. If the answer is "it looks nice," the animation is deleted.
- **Reduced motion (principle 7):** all motion has a full static fallback; nothing essential is delivered by animation (no information hidden in motion).
- **Performance (principle 8):** motion uses compositor-friendly transforms only (a Phase 2 implementation rule); the brand prefers a still-frames-strong page (Section 3.3 rationale).
- **The one signature move:** the **"before → after" reveal** — a shape/image transforms on scroll (a slip becomes a guess, a pot becomes painted). Used rarely, always for events; it becomes the brand's remembered gesture (2.4).

## 10. Spacing System

- **Density:** generous. Spacing creates luxury (principle 12). The page breathes like the hall before a show.
- **Breathing room:** identity moments get the most space; logistics get *structured* space (consistent, not cramped); nothing is ever crowded.
- **Alignment philosophy:** strict edge alignment on logistics; expressive offset on identity moments — deviation is always a decision, never sloppiness (principle 12).
- **Negative space:** the stage floor — elements stand on empty ink or empty paper like performers on a stage (2.1). Empty is not unfinished; empty is the spotlight's room.
- **Visual rhythm:** the spacing rhythm alternates **long/short** to mirror the emotional arc (2.1) — big space after a peak, compact space inside a section.
- **Section spacing:** very generous between major sections (each section is a "scene" of the festival); tight within a scene.
- **Component spacing:** consistent internal padding families (tight/normal/roomy) applied by role — cards, buttons, inputs never re-invent padding.
- **Grid thinking:** a single modular grid concept: 12 units wide, 6 tablet, 4 mobile; alignment derives from it; hero moments may break the grid deliberately (expressive offset) — and the break is itself part of the identity (2.1).

## 11. Micro Details

- **Button personality — The Stage Cue.** Chunky, generous height, amber-on-ink or ink-on-paper; hover = brightness shift + 1-unit rise; press = settle down. One shape family, one pair of colors (4.4).
- **Input personality — The Registration Desk.** Warm paper surface, ink text, clear labels above, visible focus (principle 7), errors shown with danger red + text (never color alone).
- **Card philosophy — The Slip.** Rectangular frame, paper or ink surface, one hover motion (rise + outline), imagery cropped inside the frame (Section 7). Cards are slips waiting to be picked up.
- **Links:** underlined by default on logistics; arrow-chevron affordance on identity links; hover = color shift only (never underline-trickery on large display type).
- **Hover philosophy:** hover = acknowledgement (brightness, rise, reveal), never a layout change (principle 2); on touch devices, the same states appear as focus states (principle 9).
- **Focus philosophy:** a single visible focus ring (warm amber) everywhere, keyboard-visible (principle 7). The focus ring is part of the brand, not an apology.
- **Scroll philosophy:** scroll is a scene change (2.1); scroll progress may be shown as a subtle "applause bar" on identity pages; on logistics pages, scroll is plain (2.2).
- **Loading philosophy:** instant-over-delight. Skeleton frames in paper/ink with a single amber pulse; no spinners beyond first load; long operations use a festival metaphor (a countdown "3-2-1").
- **Cursor philosophy:** the system cursor everywhere (performance, principle 8). The pointer appears only on genuinely interactive elements. No custom cursors.

## 13. The Design Constitution (Non-Negotiable, 24 principles)

1. **Every animation must tell a story** — motion is cued, never decorative.
2. **Never repeat layouts without reason** — each event page is its own mini-experience (6.5).
3. **Content creates hierarchy** — a rule, a deadline, a name; the content decides what shouts.
4. **Typography is a design element** — type performs on identity moments, explains on logistics.
5. **The loud is rationed** — applause is scarce, therefore valuable (2.1).
6. **The calm is structured** — logistics pages are calm *by design*, not by neglect (2.2).
7. **Every screen serves a primary audience** — student (emotion) or coordinator (information) (2.9).
8. **No photo, no problem** — the brand must hold without imagery (3.3, Q14).
9. **Performance is part of aesthetics** — slow is ugly (8, principle 8).
10. **Accessibility is invisible design** — AA, keyboard, reduced-motion, focus rings (7).
11. **Color is a role, not a preference** — every color has a function (4.4).
12. **Consistency beats novelty** — one identity, many surfaces (5.2.11).
13. **The before/after is the brand's memory** — transformation is told, not decorated (1.1).
14. **Nothing is added because it looks cool** — every element earns its place (12, principle 14).
15. **The frame is the crop** — all imagery lives in the signature frame (7).
16. **Numbers are honest** — tabular, aligned, never faked (5.2).
17. **Deadlines are sacred** — deadline visibility beats dead-end links (6.2).
18. **The tagline is the signature** — one signature typesetting, used once (5.2).
19. **Content stays in the rule book's words** — reworded rules are broken rules (3.3).
20. **Mobile is not a smaller desktop** — equal attention, both designed (9).
21. **Motion respects reduced preference** — nothing essential lives in animation (9).
22. **Skeletons, not spinners** — loading is part of the brand (11).
23. **The system ages well** — decisions survive 2K26 (12, principle 13).
24. **Traceability is law** — if a design can't explain its Phase 0 root, it is redesigned (Section 0).

These 24 expand Phase 0's 14 non-negotiable principles into the visual domain. Where this list is stricter, the stricter version applies.

## 14. Conceptual Design Tokens (no values, no code)

| Token group | Conceptual definition |
|---|---|
| **Color** | Categories fixed by role (Section 4.4); all surfaces derive from Ink/Paper; accents fixed by role; cluster treatments are proportional mixes, never new hues; contrast pairs defined per role pair |
| **Typography** | Three-level hierarchy (Display/Heading/Body); scale is contrast-driven (large jumps), not step-marching; numerals always tabular; one signature typesetting for the tagline only |
| **Elevation** | Flat-first: depth comes from offset stacking and outline, not shadow; levels exist conceptually (ground / slip / lifted / flying) with fixed treatments |
| **Spacing** | A base unit multiplied systematically; named cadences (tight/normal/roomy/section) applied by role; grid 12/6/4 |
| **Radius** | Semantic corners: organic (identity), small (components), zero (data) |
| **Motion** | Two speeds (cue-fast 150–250ms, pan-slow 500–700ms); two characters (elastic = celebration, linear = logistics); all motion reduced-motion-safe |
| **Shadow** | Nearly absent; only soft directional "stage cast" on lifted/flying levels; depth by stacking first, shadow last |
| **Opacity** | Used for pattern wallpaper and layered cutouts; text opacity never below AA contrast |
| **Grid** | One modular grid concept across breakpoints; hero may break it expressively |
| **Breakpoints** | Not device lists — surface classes: Identity (expressive), Cluster (mixed), Logistics (strict); responsive philosophy per surface, not per pixel |

## 15. Self-Critique (brutally honest)

**Where the identity is still weak:**

1. **The Ringmaster could be over-whimsical.** The personality leans playful; without disciplined execution it slides into "cute." Counter: the Rule Keeper voice and logistics calm are the guardrails — the brand must be *held* between the two, never free to drift.
2. **The pattern language is the real risk.** "Confetti dots, slip-edge lines, clay swatches" can become generic decoration if not drawn with a distinctive hand. The pattern vocabulary is the single most important unresolved asset — it needs dedicated exploration in Phase 2 (pattern studies, not code).
3. **Amber/gold is safe, not surprising.** It was chosen deliberately over clichés, but on its own it risks "premium warm" blandness. The distinctive work must happen in the pairing: Ink+Amber+Rose+Teal proportions and the typography, not the hues alone.
4. **Dual surfaces (Ink/Paper) could split the brand.** If cluster switching is clumsy, the site reads as two different sites. Phase 2 must prove the system holds as *one* identity across both surfaces.
5. **Numbers-centric design is under-explored.** Tabular figures and "time is sacred" are stated but not yet given personality; a display-numeral treatment (countdowns, time limits) deserves early exploration.

**What could become generic:**

- The hybrid direction could degrade into "dark hero + light sections" — the default template — if the pattern language and typography do not carry it.
- Event marks could become 16 random cliparts if the geometric-parent discipline (Section 6) is not enforced.
- Motion could become a theme of fades if the before/after signature move is not developed.

**What Phase 2 must explore (explicit list):**

1. The pattern vocabulary — 3–5 pattern studies, one selected.
2. The event mark system — the 16 marks under the geometric-parent rules.
3. Typographic signatures — display stacking, the tagline setting, numeral treatments.
4. The surface-transition proof — one page crossing Ink→Paper→Ink showing one identity.
5. Color proportion studies — cluster treatments on the same layout.
6. Voice samples — tone table (1.6) turned into real microcopy for rules, deadlines, wins.

### 15.1 What this document does not decide (left open for Phase 2)

- Exact fonts, exact hex values, exact spacing units, exact motion curves — all to be selected/proved in Phase 2 against these philosophies.
- Logo marks: the wordmark "EVOKE '2K25" exists conceptually but its drawing is a Phase 2 deliverable.
