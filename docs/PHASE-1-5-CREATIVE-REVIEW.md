## PART 1 — Critique of the Brand

### 1.1 The Electric Ringmaster persona

- **Why it works.** It is traceable (Phase 0 §1.1, 4.x), it solves the audience tension (2.9), and it gives every copywriter and designer a consistent character to channel. Personas are how agencies keep hundreds of decisions coherent.
- **Where it could fail.** The "charismatic young host" is a common archetype — talent-show, radio, festival, TEDx. The Ringmaster currently differs from that family only in *voice details*, not in *form*. If implementation channels "host energy" generically, we get the standard festival warmth — indistinguishable from a hundred sites.
- **Assumptions it depends on.** That the human writers can sustain two voices (flourish + rule-keeper) without blending them into a uniform "friendly" voice. That the audience accepts playful authority rather than finding it patronizing.
- **Does it scale?** Yes as a writing guide; it must NOT scale into mascot art. A literal illustrated ringmaster would be a brand catastrophe — he must stay a voice and a temperament, never a character graphic.
- **Originality.** Medium. The persona alone is not the differentiator. The differentiator must be structural (Part 3), not verbal.
- **Generic risk during implementation.** Very high in copy ("celebrate", "showcase your talent" — blacklist items 9, 59) and medium in imagery (posed happy students — blacklist 12). Mitigation: the tone table (Bible 1.6) must become enforceable microcopy rules, not inspiration.

### 1.2 The "before/after transformation" DNA

- **Why it works.** It is the only idea in the Bible that is genuinely *derived from the festival* rather than borrowed from design culture. Sixteen events all perform transformation (Phase 0 4.2–4.16). This is the strongest asset in the entire brand.
- **Where it could fail.** It is currently a *concept* — nothing in the Bible says how "before/after" becomes visible. Concepts without devices evaporate during implementation.
- **Assumptions.** That the transformation can be shown without photography (Bible 8.1). This is currently unproven — a real risk.
- **Does it scale?** Yes, but only if it becomes a composition device (Part 3), not just a tagline-level idea.
- **Originality.** High — no festival site we studied builds its identity on transformation as a visible device.
- **Generic risk.** If not made structural, it degrades into "workshop photos" — a craft-store aesthetic.

### 1.3 The Stage-Crafted Hybrid direction (C + A + B)

- **Why it works.** It is the only direction that survives missing photography (Q14), honors performance (Bible 8), and keeps the audience balance (2.9).
- **Where it could fail.** Three surfaces = three chances to look like three different sites. The Bible's Ink/Paper duality (4.4) and cluster treatments (4.17) reduce but do not eliminate this risk. The real safeguard is a *device that crosses all surfaces* (Part 3) — currently absent.
- **Assumptions.** That surface-switching is perceptible as "scenes of one film" rather than "mode confusion". This must be proven in Phase 2 with a single Ink→Paper→Ink journey (Bible 15.1 item 4).
- **Scales?** Yes — surfaces are a system, not a layout.
- **Originality.** Medium. "Dark + light + graphic" is common; the originality must come from the signature device and pattern language, not the surface split.
- **Generic risk.** The highest in the whole brand: without a signature device, the hybrid collapses into "dark hero, light sections, cards" — the default template (Bible 15). This review's central job is to prevent exactly that.

### 1.4 Color: Ink / Paper / Amber / Rose / Teal

- **Why it works.** Role-locked (Bible 4.4), refusals documented (4.2–4.3), functional colors defined. Amber-as-spotlight is a legitimate metaphor with both warmth and energy.
- **Where it could fail.** (a) Amber is "safe premium warm" — alone it cannot carry distinctiveness (Bible 15 admitted this). (b) The five-hue system, if proportion discipline fails, becomes a five-color brand — and five colors are always generic. (c) Teal-as-mind-cluster could silently become teal-everywhere, splitting the palette.
- **Assumptions.** That proportions (not hues) do the differentiation work. Unproven; needs the Phase 2 proportion studies.
- **Scales?** Yes, but only with the proportion rule enforced as a token, not a guideline.
- **Originality.** Low-Medium as hues; the originality must come from *where color is placed* (the reveal device, Part 3) — color as a lighting instrument rather than a fill.
- **Generic risk.** High for the accent pair (amber/rose is popular); low for the Ink surface (distinctive if kept dominant). The blacklist items 1–2 (gradients, glassmorphism) are the main drift vectors.

### 1.5 Typography: display sans + humanist sans + tabular numerals

- **Why it works.** Three-level discipline (5.1), display reserved for identity (rarity = power), tabular numerals tied to "time is sacred" (Phase 0 3.3.5). Sound.
- **Where it could fail.** Two families with no signature *setting* will look like every modern site. The tagline signature (5.2) is the only distinctive typographic moment defined — one moment is not enough to own a brand.
- **Assumptions.** That a contemporary grotesque can carry personality without becoming trendy. Font selection (Phase 2) is a genuine fork: the wrong pick ages the brand faster than any color choice.
- **Scales?** Yes.
- **Originality.** Low until the numeral language (the "festival clock") is developed — numbers are the most underused typographic asset (Bible 15 acknowledged this).
- **Generic risk.** Medium-High: "big bold sans + clean sans" is the default template recipe.

### 1.6 Motion: two speeds, two characters

- **Why it works.** Purpose rule (2), reduced-motion respect (7), performance discipline (8) — the fastest way to ruin this project would be decorative motion, and the Bible already forbids it.
- **Where it could fail.** The grammar defines *tempo and character* but not *architecture*: what moves when a section begins, what moves between pages, what never moves. Part 7 fixes this.
- **Assumptions.** That "elastic = celebration" won't be overused. Confetti bounces on every card would be a blacklist-56 violation — needs enforcement rules.
- **Scales?** Yes, with the architecture in Part 7.
- **Originality.** Low-Medium as written; the before/after reveal is the only signature move and it is currently *one* move, not a language.

### 1.7 Iconography and event marks

- **Why it works.** Geometric-parent discipline (6) prevents 16 random cliparts — a real, self-aware safeguard.
- **Where it could fail.** The mark system is still a promise. Sixteen marks that "imply transformation" is a hard drawing problem: done poorly, they are 16 different styles.
- **Assumptions.** That one system can span stage (dynamic) and craft (static) events with a single drawing language. Needs proof.
- **Scales?** Yes, if the geometric-parent rule is enforced at the grid level (one icon grid, one stroke ratio — to be specified in Phase 2).
- **Originality.** Unknowable until drawn. Flag for Phase 2 with high priority.

### 1.8 The Blacklist, the Constitution, the Tokens

- **Why they work.** They convert taste into tests. The 60-item blacklist is the project's single best defense against AI-generic implementation (Part 8 builds on it).
- **Where they could fail.** A blacklist defines what NOT to do, never what TO do. Without a positive grammar (Part 4), a junior designer can pass every blacklist item and still produce a bland site.
- **Assumptions.** That the blacklist is read and enforced in reviews — it must become part of the review checklist, not a document.
- **Scales?** Yes — it is the project's law book.
- **Originality.** N/A (it is a defense, not an identity).

**Critique summary — the three real weaknesses:**
1. The brand has *concepts* (transformation, surfaces, rationed loud) but no *device* that makes them visible. (Part 3 fixes.)
2. The brand defines *components and moods* but no *grammar* — no rules for how pages are composed. (Part 4 fixes.)
3. The brand has a blacklist (negative law) but no *positive test* for identity. (Part 9 fixes.)

## PART 2 — What is still missing?

### 2.1 The five-designer test

If the Brand Bible is handed to five senior designers, they will NOT produce the same identity. They will all produce "warm festival, dark+light, bold sans, amber" — five variations of the same *genre*, because that is exactly what the Bible specifies: a genre, honestly and well defined.

A genre is not an identity. The difference between a genre and an identity is **a device**: one recognizable structural element (or family of elements) that no one in the genre uses the same way.

### 2.2 Exactly what is missing

| # | Missing | Consequence of the gap | Where it must be fixed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A signature composition device** | Five designers → five compositions; no screenshot is recognizable | Part 3 → Appendix A |
| 2 | **A positive grammar** (how sections open/close/transition) | Pages will be assembled intuitively → template drift | Part 4 |
| 3 | **A pattern vocabulary** (the brand wallpaper) | Graphic skin can't actually be used; surfaces look empty or random | Phase 2 (already listed, Bible 15.1) |
| 4 | **A numeral/clock language** | Countdowns and time limits — the festival's core data — will be styled ad hoc | Part 3, device family |
| 5 | **An event-mark grid discipline** | 16 marks drift into 16 styles | Phase 2 (Bible 6) |
| 6 | **A logo/wordmark position** | The Bible treats "EVOKE 2K26" as text; without a lockup decision, the wordmark fights the typographic system | Phase 2 |
| 7 | **A "final" stamp treatment** | "Judges' decision is final" appears 16 times (Phase 0 3.3.1) — the single most repeated content on the site, and the Bible gives it no treatment | Part 5 |
| 8 | **Enforcement mechanics** | Nothing yet says *how* blacklist/Constitution are checked in reviews | Part 8 |

### 2.3 Which decisions are still subjective

- The exact display typeface (two plausible picks give radically different brands).
- The proportion of Ink vs Paper per page type.
- How "expressive offset" (Bible 10) is applied — one designer's expression is another's clutter.
- How the tagline signature is drawn (5.2) — the most important single typographic moment has no visual definition.
- How the before/after reveal actually looks.

All five are Phase 2 deliverables — this review does not overwrite the Bible — but Part 3 reduces their ambiguity by defining the device *family* they must all belong to.

## PART 3 — The Signature Language

> The most important part of this document. The Brand Bible gives EVOKE personality, colors, type, motion — everything except the thing that makes it recognizable with the logo removed. This part invents that thing.

### 3.1 The discovery

The brand's DNA is transformation (Bible 1.1). The festival's most repeated rule is "Judges' decision is final" (Phase 0 3.3.1). The most physical object in the rule book is **the slip** — the 10 slips of Dumb Charades, the pieces every participant brings and holds: pot, sheet, sketch, track, pen drive, carved vegetable. And the most emotional moment in any festival is **the result** — the moment the judge's verdict is delivered.

The signature language is built from **one action**: *the reveal.*

**EVOKE's signature is "THE UNVEILING"** — the site behaves as if everything is behind a curtain, a slip, a stage light, waiting to be revealed: an event name, a result, a winner, a rule, a face. Nothing is presented flat. Everything is *unveiled* — because that is what the festival does to talent: it brings it out. "Bring out the best in You" is literally a reveal action.

### 3.2 The three devices

One language, three devices — a **family**, not three separate ideas:

| Device | What it is | Why it is EVOKE | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Slip** | A small, hand-held rectangular form — like the Dumb Charades slip — that is the basic container of the site: every card, every event tile, every rule item is a slip | The slip is the rule book's most tangible object (Phase 0 3.1, event 1); it is humble, physical, held by children, and it *contains* a revelation (a word to guess, a rule to read) | All event tiles, rules lists, quick-fact rows, countdown numbers sit on slips |
| **The Curtain Edge** | The slip's edge is never a plain rounded rect: it carries a torn edge, a punched hole, a clipped corner, a taped seam — hand-made details in an otherwise precise system | It is the "craft floor" (Bible 3.2, Phase 0 4.9–4.16) meeting the stage: precision on one side, handmade on the other | Borders, dividers, section edges, event marks — the hand-detail is always on the *edge*, never the face |
| **The Unveiling** | A content block is revealed by a *stage moment* — a light sweep, a curtain pull, a stamping of a result — instead of a plain fade | The festival's emotional peak is the verdict (Phase 0 4.x "memorable moments": the qualification announcement, the buzzer ending, the winner) — the site reveals its content the same way | Page transitions, result reveals, event headers, winner announcements |

### 3.3 The signature rule (the one sentence)

> **Nothing appears on an EVOKE page without being unveiled.**

Every element enters through a reveal — light, lift, stamp, slip. This one rule, applied with discipline, makes the site recognizable from three screenshots: the *pattern* of reveals — slips rising, edges tearing, lights sweeping — is EVOKE even when the logo, colors, and fonts are removed.

### 3.4 The reveal grammar (five gestures)

The language is not "fade everything in". It is five specific gestures, each with a reason:

1. **The Sweep** — a light bar (a strip of the spotlight color) sweeps across a slip to reveal its content. Used for: hero headlines, event names, the tagline. It is the *announcement* gesture. (Bible 9: fast, confident — 150–250ms.)
2. **The Lift** — a slip rises from a stack (offset stacking, Bible 7) into place. Used for: event tiles, cards, rule items. It is the *hand-held* gesture — like picking a slip from the pile. (Bible 9: elastic on celebration, linear on logistics.)
3. **The Stamp** — the "Judges' decision is final" moment: a result text snaps into place with a hard, one-time, inky stamp (a soft, paper-print impact, not a bounce). Used for: winners, results, "final" verdicts, confirmations. It is the *verdict* gesture. (New — not in the Bible; this is the review's addition.)
4. **The Tear** — the handmade edge tears/reveals on the Art/Maker cluster: a corner of a slip lifts like paper being torn, exposing what's beneath. Used for: Art/Maker event reveals. It is the *craft* gesture.
5. **The Curtain** — a full-page or full-section transition where content is revealed as if a curtain parts (two surfaces sliding apart, not a wipe). Used for: page transitions and chapter changes. It is the *stage* gesture. (Bible 2.1: scroll is a scene change.)

### 3.5 Why this survives without logo, color, type, or text

- **Composition:** three screenshots showing slips with torn edges, a light sweep, a result stamped — no text needed to know it's a festival that unveils.
- **Motion:** the gestures (sweep/lift/stamp/tear/curtain) are behavior, not decoration — recognizable even in grayscale, even at 30fps, even in a static still if the reveal state is captured mid-gesture.
- **Negative space:** slips always sit on empty stage floor (Bible 10) — the pattern of "objects on emptiness, revealed by light" is the composition signature.
- **Cropping:** imagery is always cropped by a slip frame (Bible 15: the frame is the crop) — the slip IS the crop.

### 3.6 The pattern vocabulary (the skin of the language)

The slips need a surface to sit on — the brand wallpaper (Bible 15.1 item 1). This review commits the direction, Phase 2 draws it:

- **The stage floor:** deep Ink with a subtle, repeating motif of a faint spotlight circle at low opacity (Bible 8: pattern at low opacity).
- **The paper stack:** Paper surface with faint slip-edge lines — the ghost of stacked slips.
- **The confetti dot** — rejected as a primary motif (blacklist 10); accepted only as a *minute* detail on celebration reveals, one time, earned.
- The pattern must be drawn from slip edges, torn seams, and light rays — not generic dots, grids, or gradients.

### 3.7 The signature in one paragraph (for every future phase)

EVOKE is a festival where everything is unveiled. Every page is a stage floor of ink or paper; every piece of content is a slip; every slip has a handmade edge; every reveal is one of five gestures — sweep, lift, stamp, tear, curtain. When a visitor scrolls, clicks, or waits, something is being revealed to them — because that is what the festival does to its students. This is EVOKE's signature: **the language of the unveiling.**

## PART 4 — Visual Grammar

Like language, design has grammar. These rules define how pages are *composed* — the syntax of the unveiling.

### 4.1 Sentence structure: sections

- **A section begins with a cue, not a label.** Every major section opens with one of the five gestures (Part 3.4) — never with a static headline. The user must feel the chapter start before reading its title.
- **A section ends with a settlement.** After the section's last slip, the page *settles* (Bible 9: the page always settles) — a short, silent beat (generous spacing) before the next cue. The silence is the punctuation.
- **Sections are scenes, not blocks.** A section is a "scene of the festival" (Bible 10): it has a surface (Ink or Paper), a gesture, and a mood — it never just "continues" the previous section.
- **Chapter signal without reading:** users know a new chapter began because (a) the surface changed (Ink→Paper), (b) a cue gesture fired, and (c) the headline entered via a sweep — three signals, any one sufficient (accessibility: at least one must not depend on motion).

### 4.2 Clause structure: grouping

- **Content is grouped on slips** — one slip = one thought (one event, one rule, one fact). The slip boundary is the visual "comma"; a group of slips is a "list".
- **Groups are aligned to the grid** (Bible 10: 12/6/4) — slips align, edges align, negative space is identical. Deviation from the grid is reserved for identity moments only (Bible 10: expressive offset is a decision).
- **Two-hue limit** per surface (Bible 4.4): a section never shows more than its surface color + one accent. The grammar forbids multi-color sections.

### 4.3 Headline–imagery interaction

- **Imagery never competes with the headline.** Either the image is inside a slip (the headline sits on the stage floor beside it) or the headline is inside the slip (the image is the floor) — never both at full force.
- **The sweep reveals the headline; the lift reveals the image.** Two gestures, never simultaneous — the eye gets one event at a time (Bible 5.1: hierarchy by contrast, not shouting).
- **Text never sits on busy imagery without a scrim** (blacklist 24) — and the scrim is always the surface color, never a random translucent black.

### 4.4 How empty space behaves

- **Empty space is the stage floor** (Bible 10): it is never "filled in". If a designer feels the urge to fill negative space, that is a signal the composition is wrong, not that the space needs decoration.
- **Empty space carries the rhythm** — long/short alternation (Bible 10) — the space itself is the hush between announcements.

### 4.5 How visuals overlap

- **Overlap = layering = slips in a stack.** Overlaps are always parallel (slip on slip), never random. One slip may offset over another by a fixed small step (the "stack" offset, Bible 7: offset stacking).
- **The tearing edge overlaps** on Art/Maker: a torn corner sits over the slip beneath it — the only non-parallel overlap allowed, and it must be *torn* (handmade), never a rounded overlap.

### 4.6 How typography interrupts imagery

- **Type can perform** (Bible 5.1) but only on identity moments: a display headline may cross the slip edge, may be cropped by the frame, may sit on the stage floor. On logistics, type never leaves its slip.
- **The interruption is the reveal:** when type crosses imagery, it does so through a sweep gesture — the type is the light, the image is the floor.

### 4.7 How one section transitions into another

- **Transitions are gestures, not fades** — a curtain (chapter change), a sweep (section change), or the settlement of the previous section (no visible transition — the hush is the transition).
- **The surface change is the strongest transition** (Ink→Paper) and is reserved for chapter changes — using it more often dilutes it.
- **Motion across sections** is continuous (Bible 9: transitions create continuity) — a slip lifted in section A may continue rising into section B; nothing resets the world between sections.

### 4.8 The grammar in one sentence

> *A page is a sequence of scenes; every scene opens with a cue and closes with a hush; content lives on slips; slips stack and tear; type performs only in the spotlight; transitions are gestures; the surface change is a chapter break.*

## PART 5 — The Festival's Visual DNA

Five questions, one answer each — the DNA every future phase inherits.

### 5.1 How EVOKE frames content

**Through the slip.** Content is always *held* — by a slip, a frame, a crop. Nothing floats free. The slip is the universal container, and its handmade edge is the constant reminder that this is a children's festival, held with adult precision. (Devices: Slip, Tear.)

### 5.2 How EVOKE celebrates creativity

**By unveiling it.** Creativity is never described as "amazing" — it is *shown being revealed*: a carve is revealed by a tear, a song by a sweep. The celebration gesture is the reveal itself. (Devices: Sweep, Tear, Stamp for winners.)

### 5.3 How EVOKE expresses competition — without a sports site

**As a verdict, not a scoreboard.** Competition at EVOKE is the moment of the judge's final decision (Phase 0 3.3.1) — a stamp, a result, a name called. There are no league tables, no points, no ladders: competition is expressed as **the finale** — one decisive, stamped moment. Timers (the time limits of every event) are the *anticipation*; the stamp is the *result*. This keeps the site festive, not athletic. (Devices: Stamp, the festival clock.)

### 5.4 How EVOKE expresses performance — without a concert site

**As a stage that is also a classroom.** The stage never uses arena imagery, pyro, or crowd-noise visuals (blacklist 12, 39). Performance is expressed through **the spotlight** — a single light gesture on a dark floor — and through the discipline of the Rule Keeper: the stage is where talent is *presented*, and the rules say how. The site is a stage you could walk onto, not a stage you watch from a distance. (Devices: Sweep, Curtain, Ink surface.)

### 5.5 How EVOKE expresses art — without a gallery

**As a workshop with an audience.** Art is never presented in a sterile white gallery grid (blacklist: the "gallery look"). Art is shown *in progress* — torn edges, visible craft, the before/after reveal (Bible 13: transformation is the brand's memory). The art cluster is the one place where imperfection is a design value. (Devices: Tear, Paper surface, the before/after pair.)

### 5.6 How EVOKE expresses academics — without a school portal

**As a game of the mind.** The G.K. Quiz and Dumb Charades are expressed as *playful mental contests* — slips with words, question marks as curtain edges, the countdown as a ticking clock — never as a test, a syllabus, or a dashboard. Numbers are the festival clock, not a grade book. (Devices: Slip, the festival clock.)

### 5.7 How sixteen events still feel like one festival

**Through the constant grammar.** Sixteen events, four clusters (Phase 0 4.17), but *one* syntax everywhere: slips, edges, gestures, surfaces. The clusters change the *mood* (colors, textures, gestures), never the *grammar*. A visitor who has seen one event page can read any event page instantly — and yet each event's personality (Bible 6.5) is preserved by which gestures and edges it uses. One language, sixteen dialects.

### 5.8 The DNA statement (memorize this)

> **EVOKE holds everything in slips, reveals everything through gestures, judges everything with a stamp, and turns every page into a stage. One grammar, sixteen dialects, one festival.**

## PART 6 — Visual Motifs

Not decoration — identity. Each motif below is judged: does it belong to EVOKE, or is it forced?

### 6.1 Belongs: THE SLIP (paper, the held object)
- **Why it belongs.** It is the rule book's own object (Dumb Charades slips, Phase 0 3.1); it is held, passed, guessed — the festival's most physical artifact. It is humble (a school object) and precise (a rule carrier) — exactly the brand's two poles.
- **Rejected variants:** receipt-style slips (too commercial), lottery tickets (too gambling), tickets (too concert). It is a *word slip*, a *rule slip*, a *fact slip*.

### 6.2 Belongs: LIGHT (the spotlight)
- **Why it belongs.** The stage is the festival's heart (Phase 0 4.1–4.8); light is its universal symbol, and the sweep gesture is its behavior. Light is used as a *verb* (it reveals), never as a *wallpaper*.
- **Rejected variants:** neon, glow, gradients, rays fanning from a logo (blacklist 1, 3, 16).

### 6.3 Belongs: THE EDGE (tear, punch, seam)
- **Why it belongs.** The handmade meeting the precise — the craft clusters (4.9–4.16) are defined by hand-work with real edges. It is the only place imperfection is allowed.
- **Rejected variants:** distressed/grunge filters, random jagged edges — the tear must be *clean and intentional* (a torn paper, not a glitch).

### 6.4 Belongs: THE STAMP (verdict, finality)
- **Why it belongs.** "Judges' decision is final" — 16 times (Phase 0 3.3.1). The stamp is the only honest way to express finality without menace: it is the signature of the verdict, the applause of the rule.
- **Rejected variants:** rubber-stamp texture overuse (used once per verdict, never as page chrome), "approved"-style bureaucratic stamps.

### 6.5 Belongs: THE STACK (layering, depth)
- **Why it belongs.** Slips stack (Bible 7 offset stacking); teams huddle (Phase 0 4.6); layers are the brand's depth system — depth as *accumulation*, not as 3D.
- **Rejected variants:** glassmorphism, parallax layers, floating shadows (blacklist 2, 40).

### 6.6 Belongs: THE CLOCK (time, the countdown)
- **Why it belongs.** Every event has a time limit (Phase 0 3.3.5: time is the festival's scarcest resource). The countdown is both a real need (deadlines, timers) and a tension device (anticipation).
- **Rejected variants:** stopwatch iconography, alarm clocks, "hurry" language — the clock must feel like *anticipation*, never *pressure*.

### 6.7 Rejected: CURTAINS as a page motif
- **Why it does not belong.** Curtains belong to theatres, not to a festival that is a stage you walk onto. As a page transition gesture (the Curtain) it is useful and retained; as a *visual motif* (drawn curtain edges, curtain shapes) it is rejected — it reads theatrical, not festive, and it is a cliché of "event" design.

### 6.8 Rejected: RIBBONS, BALLOONS, CONFETTI
- **Why they do not belong.** They are the literal vocabulary of "generic school event" (blacklist 10, 18, 19, 22). Celebration at EVOKE is the stamp and the reveal — not decorations that scream "party".

### 6.9 Rejected: SOUND WAVES / MUSIC NOTES as a general motif
- **Why they do not belong.** Music is one cluster among four; a music motif on every page would lie about the festival (Phase 0 3.4: Stage/Mind/Art/Maker). Sound appears only as *rhythm* — the pace grammar — never as a drawn waveform.

### 6.10 Rejected: SPORTS ARENA / TROPHY
- **Why they do not belong.** Competition is a verdict, not a league (Part 5.3). Trophies, scoreboards, ladders, podiums — all rejected (blacklist 23).

### 6.11 The motif set (final)

**Adopted:** Slip · Light · Edge · Stamp · Stack · Clock.
**Rejected:** Curtains (as motif), Ribbons, Balloons, Confetti (except one earned moment), Sound waves, Trophies, Neon/glow, Glassmorphism.

> A designer may use *only* the adopted set. Any motif outside the set requires written approval citing which constitution clause it serves.

## PART 7 — Motion Architecture

The Bible defines motion *personality* (Bible 9: two speeds, two characters). This part defines the *architecture* — the rules developers must never guess.

### 7.1 The motion laws

1. **Nothing bounces. Nothing spins.** Elastic overshoot is reserved for the Stamp's spring-in and the Lift's settle — two gestures, no others. Rotation never occurs unless it is a literal clock hand (the festival clock).
2. **Motion begins from content, not from the frame.** Elements move *as* themselves (a slip rises, a sweep is light), never as a container, a mask, or a chrome effect. There is no background parallax; the surface is static, the slips move.
3. **Motion never distracts.** While an element animates, nothing else animates on the same axis. One motion at a time (Part 4.3).
4. **Large elements move slower; small details move faster.** The hero headline sweeps in slow (Bible 9: 500–700ms pan); a slip's edge tear is fast (150–250ms cue). Size sets tempo.
5. **The page always settles.** After any reveal, the page reaches rest within one second. No perpetual motion, no infinite loops, no ambient animation (except a live countdown clock — the festival clock — which is *functional*, not ambient).
6. **Scrolling never feels mechanical.** The scroll itself is never accelerated, decelerated, or snapped (blacklist 41: no scrolljacking). The *page's response to scroll* may be staged — reveals fire on entry — but the scrollbar always behaves naturally.
7. **Animations create anticipation.** Every reveal is preceded by a beat of stillness (the hush, Part 4.1). Anticipation is a delay *before* motion, never a buildup *during* it.
8. **Transitions create continuity.** Page transitions use the Curtain — the old page parts and the new page is revealed beneath — so the world never resets. Within a page, the previous section's last slip and the next section's first cue overlap by one beat of motion (a continuous stream).

### 7.2 The gesture map (what moves, when, how)

| Moment | Gesture | Speed | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| Page load, hero | Sweep (light bar) | Slow (pan) | Confident, one pass |
| Section entry | Cue (first slip lifts) | Fast (cue) | Linear or elastic by surface |
| Event tile hover | Lift (rise + outline) | Fast | Elastic settle |
| Result / winner / final | Stamp | Instant | Hard, paper-print impact |
| Art/Maker reveal | Tear (corner lifts) | Fast | Craft, slightly irregular |
| Chapter change | Curtain (page parts) | Slow | Continuous |
| Countdown / timer | Clock ticks | Functional | Steady, honest |
| Loading | Slip skeleton + amber pulse | Functional | Calm |

### 7.3 Motion boundaries

- **Max one signature gesture per viewport.** A page that sweeps, lifts, tears, and stamps in one screen is a page that is performing instead of informing (Bible 13.5: the loud is rationed).
- **Reduced motion** (Bible 9): every gesture has a static equivalent — sweep becomes a color change, lift becomes an outline, stamp becomes a permanent highlight, curtain becomes a surface change. Content is never delivered by motion alone (Bible 21).
- **Motion is tested for performance** (Bible 8, 9): all gestures are compositor-safe in Phase 2 implementation; a gesture that costs a frame is simplified, not justified.

## PART 8 — Generic Design Risks (where junior implementation goes wrong)

A junior developer handed the Brand Bible + this review will still drift toward generic in these specific places. Each risk names the failure and the prevention.

| # | Where generic happens | Why | Prevention (must appear in Phase 2 deliverables) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hero = centered headline on a background** | The easiest reading of "identity moment" | The hero must use the Sweep + Slip grammar; a centered static headline fails the EVOKE Test (Part 9) |
| 2 | **Cards = uniform rounded rectangles** | The universal component | Every card is a Slip: handmade edge, stack offset, one lift hover; radius only where the grammar allows (Bible 7) |

| 4 | **Gradient accents / glassy overlays** | The AI signature | Blacklist 1, 2, 16 — rejected in review by checklist, not by taste |
| 5 | **Fade-in everything** | The default animation | Fades are forbidden; every entry is one of the five gestures (Part 3.4) |
| 6 | **Standard navbar + standard footer** | The template's skeleton | Nav and footer are slips on the stage floor; the stamp and clock motifs appear here (deadline, date) — the chrome must use the language |
| 7 | **Stock photography** | The default imagery | Documentary-real only (Bible 8); before photography exists, the graphic system carries (Bible 8.1); stock is a blacklist violation |
| 8 | **A carousel** | The default "events" solution | Events are slips in a stack — scroll-revealed, never rotated (blacklist 11) |
| 9 | **Sixteen event pages, sixteen layouts** | Over-interpreting "mini-experience" (Bible 6.5) | One grammar, sixteen dialects: same syntax, different gestures/edges/colors per cluster — proven by one template + per-cluster treatments |

| 12 | **Color everywhere** | Misreading the palette | Two-hue limit per surface (Part 4.2); the palette is a system of roles, not a box of paints |

## PART 9 — The EVOKE Test

A test every future phase, page, and component must pass before it can be accepted. If any answer is the *wrong* one, the design is not unique enough.

### 9.1 The test

| # | Question | Pass = |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Could this belong to another festival? | NO — it uses the slip/gesture/edge language another festival would not use |
| 2 | Could this belong to any event website? | NO — no generic card-grid, no generic hero, no generic fade |
| 3 | Would removing the logo make the identity disappear? | NO — the reveal grammar and slip composition remain recognizable |
| 4 | Could another school reuse this layout unchanged? | NO — the layout *is* the language; swapping text would break the grammar |
| 5 | Would a designer recognize EVOKE from only three screenshots? | YES |
| 6 | Does every page contribute to one coherent identity? | YES — same grammar, cluster-scaled mood |
| 7 | Is the loud rationed? | YES — one signature gesture per viewport, hushed logistics |
| 8 | Does every element pass the blacklist and the constitution? | YES |
| 9 | Does every animation answer "what is it telling?" | YES |
| 10 | Would the page survive its photos being removed? | YES — the graphic system carries (Bible 8.1) |
| 11 | Does the page use only adopted motifs? | YES — Slip, Light, Edge, Stamp, Stack, Clock |
| 12 | Is the calm structured, not empty? | YES — logistics surfaces are deliberately composed, not abandoned |

### 9.2 How to use the test

- **Every phase deliverable** (wireframes, motion, UI, development) runs the test in review.
- **Two or more failures = redesign** (the blacklist rule, Bible 12.1, extended to the test).
- **The test is the gatekeeper between phases:** nothing enters the next phase without passing.
- **The test can be tightened, never loosened**, without written approval citing which clause is served.

## PART 10 — Appendix A: The Signature Language (mandatory reading)

> This appendix is the enforceable summary of Part 3. Future phases — UX, wireframes, motion, UI, development — must reference it before making any visual decision. It is the project's third constitution.

### A.1 The definition

**EVOKE's signature is THE UNVEILING:** the site behaves as if everything is behind a curtain, a slip, or a stage light, waiting to be revealed. Nothing is presented flat; everything is unveiled — because that is what the festival does to its students ("Bring out the best in You" is a reveal action).

### A.2 The three devices

1. **The Slip** — the universal container. Every card, tile, rule item, and fact is a slip: a small hand-held rectangular form, humble and precise.
2. **The Edge** — the handmade detail: torn edge, punched hole, clipped corner, taped seam. Precision on the face, craft on the edge. Art/Maker clusters may tear; Stage/Mind keep clean clipped edges.
3. **The Unveiling** — content enters through a stage moment, never a plain fade.

### A.3 The five gestures (the complete motion vocabulary)

1. **Sweep** — light bar sweeps across a slip to reveal. Announcements, headlines, tagline. Slow, confident.
2. **Lift** — a slip rises from a stack into place. Cards, tiles, rule items. Fast.
3. **Stamp** — a result snaps in with one inky, paper-print impact. Verdicts, winners, finals, confirmations. Instant, once, never bounced.
4. **Tear** — a handmade corner lifts to reveal. Art/Maker events only. Fast, slightly irregular.
5. **Curtain** — a section/page parts and the next is revealed beneath. Chapter and page transitions. Slow, continuous.

### A.4 The rules (non-negotiable)

- Nothing appears without being unveiled.
- One signature gesture per viewport.
- Two-hue limit per surface; surfaces are Ink or Paper.
- Content lives on slips; slips stack; edges tear only where allowed.
- Type performs only in identity moments, via the sweep.
- The festival clock (tabular numbers, honest countdowns) is the numeral language.
- The stamp is the only way a verdict is expressed.
- Only six motifs exist: Slip, Light, Edge, Stamp, Stack, Clock.
- Reduced motion: every gesture has a static equivalent; content is never delivered by motion alone.
- The loud is rationed: logistics surfaces are hushed; heroes are not louder, they are more revealed.

### A.5 The signature test

A page is EVOKE if: the composition shows slips on a stage floor; at least one reveal gesture is visible mid-flight in the first viewport; the result moments stamp; and the identity survives the logo being removed — three screenshots, one festival.

## PART 11 — Negative Grammar, Slip Universality, Visual Rhythm (addendum from the review session)

*Added after the Phase 1.5 review session. This part refines Parts 3–7; it contradicts nothing above.*

### 11.1 Negative grammar — when to intentionally break our own rules

Rules protect the identity; named exceptions make it breathe. An exception must be **named before it is used** — propose, approve, document — never improvised by a designer mid-build.

| # | Named exception | Breaks | Purpose | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Opening Act** — homepage hero | normal spacing rhythm (Part 4.4) | marks the threshold between outside and festival; the widest hush on the site | hero only |
| 2 | **The Stamp spring-in** | the no-bounce law (Part 7.1.1) | a verdict needs exactly one impact | result moments only |
| 3 | **The Tear** | precision edges (Bible 7) | craft clusters are hand-made | Art/Maker only |
| 4 | **The festival clock** | nothing-perpetual (Part 7.1.5) | countdowns are functional, never ambient | timers/countdowns |
| 5 | **The lost slip** — 404 / error pages | the entire grammar, deliberately | the only page allowed to be offstage: a torn, empty slip — "this slip got lost" | error pages |
| 6 | **The waiting slip** — empty states | "every slip has content" | negative space *is* the message: this slip waits for your name | empty states, form completion |

**Rule:** breaking a rule is a *moment*, not a permission. If the visitor can't remember the moment, it wasn't an exception — it was a mistake.

### 11.2 The slip universality test — is it branding or decoration?

A device that only wraps content is decoration. A device that can carry **every layer** of the experience is branding. The slip was tested across all eight surfaces:

| Surface | The slip exists as | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Motion | the mover — sweep, lift, tear are slip behaviors | PASS |
| Lighting | the spotlight — a slip on the Ink surface is *lit* | PASS |
| Navigation | nav items = slips in a horizontal stack; active = lifted | PASS |
| Transitions | the curtain = stacked slips parting | PASS |
| Empty space | the stage floor; the waiting slip (11.1.6) | PASS |
| Loading | the slip drawing itself — skeleton = slip outline + amber ink fill | PASS |
| Forms | inputs = slips waiting for the pen | PASS |
| Error pages | the torn, lost slip (11.1.5) | PASS |

**Conclusion:** the slip is real branding — it exists at every layer, and the pen (cursor, focus) is its tool. Any future surface that **cannot** be expressed as a slip must explain why, in writing, before it is built.

### 11.3 Visual rhythm — tempo, not just grammar

Grammar says how scenes compose; tempo says how they breathe. The festival rhythm:

**fast → slow → quiet → dense → open → impact → silence**

- **Beat alternation:** no two consecutive sections share the same tempo; the alternation *is* the rhythm.
- **Density is content-typed** (the basis of the Phase 2 Information Density Map): hero = almost no text; about = slightly more; events = rich; rules/logistics = calm, structured density; registration = simple again; footer = minimal silence.
- **The hush is a tempo, not an absence:** silence sections (footer, chapter gaps) are composed like every other beat.
- **Reading rhythm, not animation rhythm:** this map governs how much the eye is asked to do per section; motion (Part 7) follows it, never fights it.

### 11.4 The one last rule (binds every future phase)

> **Nothing may be designed because it looks good. Every visual decision must answer: what user problem or emotional goal does this solve? If no answer exists, remove it.**

This rule sits above the EVOKE Test: the Test catches the generic; this rule catches the gratuitous. It is part of this constitution and of AGENTS.md.
