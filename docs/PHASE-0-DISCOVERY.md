## 1. Project Understanding

### 1.1 What is this festival?

EVOKE 2K26 is an **inter-school festival** — a competitive and creative gathering where students from multiple schools compete across 16 events spanning performance (stage) and artistic/craft (off-stage) disciplines. The rule book officially divides the programme into **On-Stage** and **Off-Stage** events. The tagline — *"Bring out the best in You"* — is the festival's core promise: it is not just a contest, it is a platform for students to discover and display talents they do not usually get to show inside a classroom.

### 1.2 What purpose does it serve?

1. **A celebration of student talent** — music, dance, art, craft, intellect, and creativity in one venue.
2. **A school community event** — schools compete, but the tone of the rules (modesty, decency, originality) suggests a values-driven, community-building event rather than a purely commercial one.
3. **A bridge between academic identity and creative identity** — events like G.K. Quiz sit alongside Fashion Show and Best Out of Waste, signaling that "smart" and "creative" students are equally celebrated.
4. **A marketing/institution-building instrument** — inter-school events position the hosting school and its student committee as organisers of quality experiences (visible in the professional rule book structure).
5. **A repeatable annual ritual** — the rule book's "2K25" implies an edition-based structure ("2K24", "2K26" exist/follow), meaning the platform should feel like an annual institution, not a one-off landing page.

### 1.3 Why would schools participate?

- **Reputation**: Winning schools gain prestige; participation itself signals an active, holistic school culture.
- **Student growth**: Cheap, low-risk exposure to stage confidence, competition, and creative discipline.
- **Bonding**: Teams travel together; teachers and students share an experience outside the classroom.
- **Showcase**: A platform to show off their best students to other schools, parents, and the host institution.
- **Zero/low cost**: The rule book requires participants to bring their own materials (pots, vegetables, tracks, instruments) — i.e., schools only invest transport and supervision.

### 1.4 Why would students become excited?

- **Performance is thrilling**: Dancing, singing, acting on a real stage with a live audience.
- **Novelty**: Face painting, vegetable carving, cooking without fire, pot painting — these are not things students do in school every day.
- **Friendship teams**: Most events are 2–8 member teams — the experience is social, not solitary.
- **Recognition**: Judges, certificates (implied by rule book's "Judges' decision is final"), applause, and the chance to be "the best in you".
- **Identity**: A festival is one of the few places where a shy artist or a quiet quizzer becomes the star.

### 1.5 What emotions should the website create?

| Emotion | Where it lives |
|---|---|
| **Anticipation / excitement** | Countdown energy, event reveals, "can you picture yourself here?" |
| **Pride / belonging** | School names, past winners, gallery moments, the festival's "we are one stage" feeling |
| **Curiosity** | Events with intriguing names (Best Out of Waste, Dumb Charades) that invite exploration |
| **Clarity / reassurance** | Rules, eligibility, dates, venue, registration steps — parents and coordinators must feel *safe* |
| **Joy / playfulness** | Youthful, vibrant, warm; it is a school festival, not a corporate conference |
| **Aspiration** | "Bring out the best in You" — visitors should finish feeling *they* could be on that stage |

### 1.6 What experience should users have?

- A **student** should feel the festival before they read it — music in the visual mood, stages in the layout, colour in the tone — and should be able to find "what can I do?" in under 30 seconds.
- A **coordinator/teacher** should land on complete, unambiguous logistics: dates, venue, deadlines, per-school team limits, materials lists, contact person.
- A **parent** should quickly understand what the event is, whether it is safe/appropriate (the rule book's emphasis on decency supports this), and how to support their child.
- An **organizer** should have an information system that is editable, accurate, and scalable — this year and next.

### 1.7 What should they remember after leaving?

- The **feeling**: colour, energy, youth, "I want to be there."
- The **tagline**: "Bring out the best in You" — short enough to carry.
- The **events**: at least the one that belongs to *them* ("Group Dance — that's our thing!").
- The **next step**: exactly how to register and the deadline — no ambiguity left behind.

**Audience priority ranking for design decisions:** Participant Students (1) > Coordinators/Teachers (2) > Parents (3) > Organizers (4) > Judges (5) > Future participants (6) > Sponsors (7) > Public (8). Design must serve students emotionally and coordinators operationally — these two are in tension and must be balanced, not traded off.

**Focus rule (do not optimize for "everyone"):** Design primarily for **Students (emotion)** and **Coordinators (information)**. Everyone else is secondary. This is how the product stays focused — every screen is either emotionally magnetic for a student or operationally precise for a coordinator. If a design element serves neither, it does not belong.

### 3.2 Categorization matrix

| Axis | On-Stage (1–8) | Off-Stage (9–16) |
|---|---|---|
| **Performance vs Craft** | Performance (7 of 8 are performative; Quiz is intellectual) | Craft/Artistic (7 of 8; Cooking is culinary-creative) |

| **Skill-based** | Dumb Charades (acting + word knowledge), Instrumental (musicianship) | All craft events are skill-based (dexterity, aesthetics) |

### 3.3 Patterns across events

1. **"Judges' decision is final" appears in every single event** — a universal, non-negotiable pillar. The website's tone must communicate fairness without defensiveness.
2. **Bring-your-own-materials is universal** for off-stage events; bring-your-own-track is standard for dance/song. "What do I bring?" is the single most repeated user question.
3. **Decency/originality clauses recur**: vulgar music prohibited (Group Dance), decent songs (Solo Song), modest clothing (Fashion Show), original work or disqualification (Art & Craft, Collage, Sketch). The festival protects a wholesome brand — the website should reflect that values-conscious tone.
4. **On-spot themes** in Collage and Pencil Sketch ("theme will be announced on the spot") — these two events cannot be prepared in advance; an important expectation-setting detail for participants.
5. **Time limits are the dominant structural rule** — nearly every event has one, ranging 1 minute to 2 hours. Time is the festival's scarcest resource and its most emotional (rushing performers).
6. **Team-size caps are the main eligibility rule** — 2 max per school (Quiz), 2 per class (Group Dance), and specific team ranges elsewhere.
7. **Two-phase format exists only in G.K. Quiz** (written prelims → oral finals) — a unique "rounds" structure.
8. **Rules are stated as prohibitions more than permissions** — the rule book tells you what you may NOT do. The website must translate this into friendly, positive guidance without losing legal accuracy.

### 3.4 Differences / groupings worth exploiting

| Cluster | Events | Shared personality |
|---|---|---|
| **The Stage Glamour cluster** | Group/Solo Song, Group/Solo Dance, Fashion Show, Instrumental Music | Lights, sound, applause, drama, costumes |
| **The Mind cluster** | G.K. Quiz, Dumb Charades | Quick thinking, wordplay, competition of the brain |
| **The Art cluster** | Face Painting, Pot Painting, Pencil Sketch, Art & Craft, Collage | Quiet, focused, visual, gallery-like |
| **The Maker cluster** | Vegetable Carving, Cooking Without Fire, Best Out of Waste | Hands-on, tactile, "wow, they made that?" |
| **The Showcase cluster** | All — the festival is itself a showcase of every cluster | — |

## 5. Competitor Research (categories, principles only — no copying)

### 5.1 What was studied

Award-winning event sites (Webby 2026 winners/nominees, Awwwards/FWA/FWA-class sites), festival websites (Sónar, Roskilde, Lowlands, Shambhala, NOS Alive, Newport Folk, Boston Calling), technology/creative conferences (Web Summit, CES, Dreamforce, Google I/O, SXSW, TED, Config/Figma, Adobe MAX, TechCrunch Disrupt, HubSpot INBOUND, Cannes Lions, Sundance, Sydney Film Festival, NYFF), sports events (Boston Marathon, Bank of America Chicago Marathon), and product launches.

### 5.2 Why the best are memorable — extracted principles

1. **One idea, executed everywhere** (Art direction). Award-winning sites are not decorated templates; every type choice, colour, and layout serves a single point of view (TED's minimalism, Lowlands' algorithmic playfulness, Roskilde's "lights in the night"). *→ For EVOKE: "Bring out the best in You" and festival energy must be one coherent idea across every screen.*
2. **Directed motion, not decoration.** Transitions carry meaning; scroll sequences pace a story; micro-interactions reward attention (INBOUND's motion storytelling, Roskilde's light-guided reveals). Motion must have a director.
3. **Performance is part of the craft.** Jurors test on real mid-range phones; beauty at 60fps is the discipline. A 9MB page is a failure no matter how pretty.
4. **The 5-second test.** Within ~5 seconds a first-time visitor can say: what it is, when, where, how to register. (Web Summit, SaaStr, Config.) Hero = 3 W's + one dominant action.
5. **Clarity beats cleverness.** One dominant CTA, scannable hierarchy, whitespace (TED, Bloomberg Live, Boston Marathon).
6. **Immersive ≠ confusing.** Cinematic atmospheres (Dreamforce, Tomorrowland, Boston Calling) still keep line-ups, dates, and tickets one click away. Mood and action coexist: "atmosphere-first hero + hard-working info bar."
7. **Audience-segmented architecture.** CES and Web Summit route by "who you are" (attendee, exhibitor, press, sponsor) before dumping everything on one visitor.
8. **Event-phase state awareness.** The best sites change their job over time: pre-event (announce, register) → live (schedule) → post (archive, winners, "next year" handoff). Stale registration pages and wrong-year links are a classic failure (Web Summit's stale schedule example).
9. **Social proof near the CTA.** Attendee numbers, school names, winners, sponsor logos, testimonials — placed where the decision happens.
10. **Accessibility and inclusivity are credibility.** Contrast, keyboard navigation, screen-reader support, reduced-motion paths — in 2026 these are baseline expectations, not extras.
11. **Mobile is the primary device** (65%+ of event traffic). Single-scroll, responsive CTAs, compressed media, no pinch-zoom requirements.
12. **Scalable systems, not one-off pages.** Modular content blocks (Sónar's "library of modules") let organizers change content quickly; headless/content-managed approaches keep sites up 100% (AEG/BST Hyde Park) and survive peak traffic (Sónar: 20–30K concurrent).

### 5.3 How they avoid overwhelming visitors

- **Progressive disclosure**: headline → category → details; never all content on one screen.
- **One action per screen**: a page asks you to do one thing.
- **Visual chunking**: cards, consistent grids, predictable navigation (Dreamforce's modular blocks).
- **Filtering and sorting**: multi-track events give users control (Sónar's filterable timetable).
- **Two-tier navigation**: main menu = essential festival info; global menu = secondary (galleries, archives) (Sónar's IA pattern).

### 5.4 How they build excitement

- Countdowns and "season" staging (announce → build → sell → live → recap).
- Reveal pacing: line-ups/events revealed over time (Lowlands, festival season models).
- Emotional media: real attendee moments, behind-the-scenes, past highlights.
- Community signals: who's coming, who won, what the crowd felt.
- A clear "see yourself here" narrative (Dreamforce: "when visitors can see themselves at your event, conversion follows").

### 5.5 What EVOKE should borrow (principles, not layouts)

- A single dominant emotional idea + a relentless 3 W's (What/When/Where) hero
- Mood-first visuals with a hard-working info system underneath
- Audience-aware paths (student / teacher / parent / organizer)
- Event-phase lifecycle: Announce → Explore → Register → Attend → Recap
- Winners + gallery as social proof (the strongest asset EVOKE can own)
- Performance discipline and mobile-first thinking baked in from the start
- Modular content so next year's edition is an upgrade, not a rewrite

## 6. Information Architecture

### 6.1 Proposed pages and their purpose (no design — structure only)

| Page | Purpose | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|
| **Home** | Identity, energy, the 3 W's, top-level navigation, featured events, latest news | The front door; must answer What/When/Where in 5 seconds |
| **About the Festival** | What EVOKE is, tagline, mission, host school, history/editions | Builds trust and identity for parents, sponsors, newcomers |
| **Events (browse)** | Full catalogue grouped by On-Stage / Off-Stage clusters | The core discovery surface; students explore "what can I do?" |
| **Event detail** | One page per event: rules, team size, time limit, theme, materials to bring, judging criteria — see the mini-experience structure below | The operational contract for coordinators and participants |
| **Schedule** | Day-wise/event-wise timeline | Logistics; when and where each event happens **[requires data]** |
| **Registration** | Team registration flow with school quota rules | The conversion point — the festival's most important operation |
| **Rules & Eligibility** | General rules, per-school limits, decency/originality policy | Answers coordinator questions without hunting |
| **Venue / Reach Us** | Address, directions, map, contact | Logistics for schools, parents, media |
| **Winners / Gallery** | Results, photos, highlights (post-event; feeds next year) | Social proof + future-participant magnet |
| **Contact** | Organizer contact, queries, committee | Every audience eventually needs a human |
| *(Admin)* | Organizer-facing management of content and registrations | Operational necessity — internal only |

### 6.2 What belongs where (hierarchy logic)

- **Homepage**: festival identity, tagline, dates, venue, single dominant action (register/explore), 2–3 featured events, latest announcement, social proof. **Never** on homepage: full rule text, full event-by-event details, sponsor proposal PDFs, admin.
- **Second level** (one click): Events, Schedule, Registration, About, Contact.
- **Third level** (details): individual event pages, rules FAQ, registration help, gallery.
- **Information priority for every audience**: Date/venue (1) → What events (2) → Am I eligible + what do I bring (3) → How do I register + deadline (4) → Schedule (5) → Everything else.

### 6.3 Navigation model (concept)

- **Primary nav**: Home · Events · Schedule · Registration · About · Contact (+ Gallery/Winners when available)
- **Audience switcher**: "For Students / For Schools / For Parents" — deep-link paths into the same content, re-sorted by that audience's priority (CES/Web Summit pattern)
- **Event clusters**: On-Stage / Off-Stage as top-level grouping inside Events; clusters (Stage/Mind/Art/Maker) as friendly sub-grouping

### 6.4 Content states the site must support (lifecycle)

1. **Announcement** — site reveals, teasers, countdown
2. **Registration open** — forms live, deadlines visible
3. **Live** — schedule mode, venue info, day-of updates
4. **Post-event** — winners, gallery, thanks, "see you next year"

Each state must be explicit; stale states are the #1 failure in event sites (see 5.2/5.3).

### 6.5 Event page: not a form, a mini-experience

Each event page is its own experience with personality — the page for Group Dance and the page for Pencil Sketch should not feel like the same page filled with different text. The event's cluster personality (Section 4) shapes its visual telling. A single, consistent information sequence covers all events:

1. **Introduction** — a short, warm, one-paragraph story of the event
2. **Quick facts** — team size, duration, stage/off-stage
3. **Theme** — where one exists (Fashion, Veg Carving, Pot Painting, Collage, Pencil Sketch)
4. **Participation** — who can join, team format, school/class quota rules
5. **Experience** — what it feels like to take part (energy and vibe, from Section 4)
6. **Rules** — official, worded faithfully to the rule book (never reworded in a way that changes meaning)
7. **Judging criteria** — exactly what judges evaluate
8. **Things to bring** — the universal and event-specific materials lists
9. **FAQ** — the recurring coordinator/student questions for this specific event
10. **Register** — the CTA and any per-event registration notes

This gives each event discoverable depth without burying the rules — the emotional part breathes, the operational part stays scannable.

## 7. User Journeys (structure only, no UI)

### 7.1 Student discovering events
- **Start**: Share link from WhatsApp/Instagram/school notice → Home
- **Decision points**: "What can I do?" → events list → pick cluster (dance? art? brains?) → event detail → rules check ("can I bring a friend? what time?")
- **Possible confusion**: Team-size caps ("max 2"), on-spot themes, "minimum 8 correct to qualify", needing own track — each is an expectation-setter that must be visible on the event page
- **Ideal experience**: 3 clicks from Home to "I know my event, my rules, my materials, and how to register"
- **Ending**: Registration completed + confirmation received; student shares it with friends

### 7.2 Teacher / coordinator checking rules
- **Start**: Direct link (printed QR / school notice) → Home or Events
- **Decision points**: Which events → team limits per school/class → deadlines → materials lists → registration batch
- **Possible confusion**: G.K. Quiz max 2 teams per school; Group Dance max 2 per class; on-spot theme events can't be prepped
- **Ideal experience**: A printable/official rules view, per-school quota clarity, one registration path with confirmation
- **Ending**: All teams registered, rules exported/printed, students briefed

### 7.3 Parent evaluating the festival
- **Start**: Child's excitement / school notice → Home
- **Decision points**: What is this? → Is it safe/appropriate (decency clauses help) → When/where → Cost **[MISSING]** → What does my child bring
- **Possible confusion**: Missing fees, missing supervision info
- **Ideal experience**: Reassurance in the About, clarity in logistics, contact available
- **Ending**: Confidence → child attends → parent becomes audience

### 7.4 Coordinator registering teams (organizational)
- **Start**: Registration page → school info → team-by-team entry → quota validation (per-school, per-class limits) → review → submit
- **Decision points**: Editing a team, adding late entries, deadline enforcement
- **Possible confusion**: Duplicate teams, wrong quotas, unclear confirmation
- **Ideal experience**: Batched entry, instant quota feedback, confirmation + receipt/email
- **Ending**: Confirmed registration + scheduled event slots

### 7.5 Organizer managing content
- **Start**: Admin login → dashboard of state (registration count, schedule, content)
- **Decision points**: Update rules/venue/timings → publish announcements → view registrations → export
- **Possible confusion**: Editing without technical skill; content going stale
- **Ideal experience**: Simple content editing, version certainty, publish preview
- **Ending**: Accurate site at every lifecycle phase

### 7.6 Judge preparing for an event
- **Start**: Event detail page (from organizer's link) → rules + criteria + format + schedule
- **Decision points**: Confirming format (e.g., Quiz prelims vs oral), time limits, judging criteria
- **Ideal experience**: One page with everything their event needs
- **Ending**: Prepared judge, fair festival

### 7.7 Future participant / visitor after the event
- **Start**: Search/social → Gallery or Winners
- **Decision points**: What happened? Who won? Is there a next edition?
- **Possible confusion**: Dead links, expired registration CTAs
- **Ideal experience**: Post-event state with winners, gallery, "see you at EVOKE '2K26" handoff
- **Ending**: FOMO → follows the festival → registers next year

## 12. Non-Negotiable Principles

This is the rulebook for every later phase. Whenever a new idea, design, or implementation is proposed, it must be checked against this list. If a proposal violates a principle, the proposal is redesigned — the principle is not bent.

1. **The website should never feel like an admin dashboard.** It is the face of a festival — every screen must carry energy, even the most practical ones.
2. **Every animation must have purpose.** Motion tells the festival story; it is never decoration. If a movement does not serve emotion or clarity, it is removed.
3. **No generic AI sections.** Every section is written for EVOKE 2K26: its events, its audiences, its tone. Nothing reads like a template.
4. **No repeated layouts.** Each event page is a mini-experience with its own personality (Section 6.5); clusters and screens do not copy stickers over each other's structure.
5. **No placeholder content.** Nothing ships that says "TBD", "Lorem", or uses invented rules. Real rules come from the rule book; missing data waits for the organizer.
6. **Content drives design.** A design is only as good as the real event content it carries. Structure is chosen after the content is understood, never before.
7. **Accessibility is required.** WCAG AA, keyboard-complete, screen-reader compatible, reduced-motion supported — not a nice-to-have.
8. **Performance is a feature.** Core Web Vitals (LCP, CLS < 0.1, INP < 200 ms) are acceptance criteria, tested on mid-range mobile devices.
9. **Desktop and mobile receive equal attention.** Mobile is primary (65%+ of traffic), but desktop is not an afterthought — both are designed deliberately.
10. **Every page tells part of the festival story.** The site is one continuous narrative: emotion first, information second, action third.
11. **Consistency is more important than novelty.** Reusing a good pattern across pages beats a new clever idea for its own sake.
12. **Visual hierarchy is intentional.** Every screen has a clear "what to look at first, then what, then what" — decided in design, not arrived at by accident.
13. **The website should age well.** It is built to evolve into EVOKE '2K26 without a redesign; today's decisions never paint the site into a corner.
14. **Nothing is added simply because it looks cool.** Every element earns its place by serving an audience (Students = emotion, Coordinators = information, or the festival story).
