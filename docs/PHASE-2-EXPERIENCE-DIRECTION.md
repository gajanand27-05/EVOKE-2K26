## PART 1 — The Participant Story

### 1.1 One participant

Aarav, 15, class 10. He first hears the word "EVOKE" from a WhatsApp broadcast a classmate forwards — a poster carrying the tagline *"Bring out the best in You."* He has never participated in an inter-school event. He wants to try something, but he does not know what he is allowed to do, whether his school is participating, whether it will cost anything, or whether he would embarrass himself.

**Before he opens the platform, he is:**

- **Curious** — the poster's promise sounds big.
- **Uncertain** — "is this for someone like me?"
- **Slightly afraid** — performing in front of strangers.
- **In need of permission** — "can I actually do this?"

**His unspoken questions, in order:**

1. What is EVOKE, actually?
2. Is it real and official — can I trust it?
3. Do I belong here — can someone like me participate?
4. What can I do there?
5. Which event fits me?
6. What exactly is expected of me in that event?
7. How do I register, and what happens after?
8. Will I know what's happening next?

*(Phase 0 2.1: student goals, frustrations, and questions; Phase 0 2.9 focus rule — students emotionally first.)*

### 1.2 The emotional journey, moment by moment

| Moment | He thinks | Emotion | Confidence | What must be true |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Poster / word of mouth | "What is this?" | Curious, wary | Low | The promise is honest |
| 2. Arrival | "Am I in the right place?" | Calm, oriented | Rising | It is unmistakably EVOKE; the tagline greets him |
| 3. Identity beat | "A festival of talent. For students like me." | Relieved, curious | Rising | Who it is for is stated immediately |
| 4. The pulse | "Wow — look what people make." | Inspired | Medium | A transformation moment touches him |
| 5. The landscape | "Sixteen events. Which one is mine?" | Excited, choosing | Medium | The landscape is scannable, never overwhelming |
| 6. One event catches him | "Dumb Charades. I'd be good at that." | Excited, hopeful | Rising | Each event has a personality, not just a name |
| 7. The rules | "Team of two. Ten slips. Eight correct to win. That's me." | Focused, certain | High | Rules are exact, honest, complete |
| 8. The decision | "Yes. I'm doing this." | Committed | High | Choice is confirmed before any form begins |
| 9. Registration | "This is all they need. Clear." | Confident | High | Nothing asked was unexpected |
| 10. Confirmation | "I'm in. And I know what happens next." | Triumphant, safe | Highest | Confirmation is immediate and concrete |
| 11. Later | "I'll check back for updates." | Anticipatory | Stable | The platform has a home for him to return to |

*(Charter §5 Progressive Confidence: at every step he is more certain than before. Phase 1 §2.1 emotional arc 0–3s → after leaving.)*

### 1.3 His decision points (where he could stop)

- After arrival, if the platform cannot tell him what EVOKE is in one honest beat.
- After the landscape, if no event feels like it was written for someone like him.
- At the rules, if reading them feels like work or raises new questions.
- At the decision, if he is asked something he was never told about.
- At payment (TBD), if he feels rushed or the process is opaque.
- After confirmation, if nothing tells him what happens next.

### 1.4 His recovery moments (where doubt is repaired)

- Doubt: "Is this official?" → Repair: credible identity and a real contact path (Q12 pending) present, quietly, where doubt forms.
- Doubt: "Am I eligible?" → Repair: eligibility is answered at the event — before the doorway, never at the form (Part 10).
- Doubt: "What if I can't form a team?" → Repair: the event states team requirements and what solo or small-team participants do (per official rules — TBD).
- Doubt: "Did I miss a rule?" → Repair: the moment of commitment restates the essentials.
- Doubt: "What now?" → Repair: the journey always ends with a next step, never with silence (Part 12, Part 14).

> **The psychological law of this part:** every answer must raise the next question, and every question must be answered before it becomes a reason to leave. *(Phase 0 1.7: what they should remember — the feeling, the tagline, their event, the next step.)*

**Trace:** Phase 0 §1.5–1.7, §2.1, §2.9, §12.3/12.10; Phase 1 §1.6, §2.1; Charter §3–§5.

## PART 2 — The Experience Narrative

### 2.0 The film frame

The platform is a short film with five acts. The visitor is not the audience — the visitor is the **protagonist walking onto the stage** (Phase 1 §1.1: the Ringmaster turns each person in the audience into a performer). The festival is the world; the participant's confidence is the plot (Charter §1: the identity is the vehicle, registration is the journey).

### 2.1 The five acts

| | **Act I — The Call** | **Act II — The Festival** | **Act III — The Rules** | **Act IV — The Decision** | **Act V — The Afterglow** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Establish what EVOKE is and who it is for | Reveal the festival's world and its sixteen stages | Make participation concrete and honest | Convert certainty into commitment | Make the registered participant certain about what comes next |
| **Emotional goal** | Curiosity + safety: "I want to know more, and I trust this" | Wonder + belonging: "there is a place for me here" | Calm + focus: "I know exactly what's expected" | Confidence + pride: "I'm doing this" | Anticipation + security: "I'm going, and I'm prepared" |
| **Information goal** | The promise, the tagline, one honest statement of what EVOKE is | The event landscape: sixteen events, four natures (Phase 0 §4.17) | Full rules: eligibility, teams, materials, time, judging | What registration requires; payment facts (TBD) | Confirmation, next steps, how updates reach the participant |
| **Desired behaviour** | Stay and look further | Select an event to examine | Confirm fit — or leave informed | Complete registration | Return for updates; share; invite others |
| **Transition** | An invitation — the promise ends with "come see" | A handoff — from "look at the festival" to "this could be yours" | A calm shift — energy settles so the mind can work | A doorway — commitment begins with a restatement of choice | An open horizon — the platform hands the future back to the participant |
| **Momentum** | Rising, gentle | Rising to a peak | Settling, steady | Focused, decisive | Steady, warm |
| **Ending condition** | "I understand what EVOKE is." | "I found the event I want." | "I know exactly what's expected." | "I'm registered." | "I know what happens now — I'll return." |

### 2.2 Why five acts

- **Acts I–II feed curiosity and belonging** — the festival's heart (Phase 0 §4; Phase 1 §1.1 transformation DNA; Phase 1.5 Part 11.3 rhythm: fast → quiet).
- **Act III is where confidence is built with facts** — content drives design (Phase 0 12.6); missing data is honestly missing (Phase 0 §10; Charter §7).
- **Act IV is the point of the platform** (Charter §1): registration completed with certainty.
- **Act V is the return engine** — announcements as the source of truth (Phase 0 12.5), the participant's space, the annual-institution feel (Phase 0 1.2.5).

Act III is deliberately slower than Act II — not to frustrate, but because (Charter §5) confidence is only real when it is earned from rules, not sold by energy.

### 2.3 Emotional pacing rules (from Act I to Act V)

1. **Energy and certainty alternate:** Act II peaks energy; Act III peaks certainty; Act IV peaks commitment; Act V restores warmth. (Phase 1 §2.3: intentional valleys are designed, not accidental.)
2. **No act asks for two strong emotions at once** (Phase 1.5 Part 4.3: one event at a time).
3. **Every act ends at exactly the moment the next act's first beat becomes a question** (Part 5, the Curiosity Curve).
4. **The loud is rationed** (Bible 13.5): the five acts are not five explosions — two peaks and a focused plateau (Phase 1 §2.2: Peak 1, Valley 1, Peak 2, Peak 3).

**Trace:** Phase 0 §1.5, §2.9, §12.10–12.11; Phase 1 §2.1–2.4; Phase 1.5 Part 4, Part 11.3; Charter §1–§5.

## PART 3 — The Chapter Map

### 3.1 The story's chapters (in order)

The platform is a story; every chapter has a role in it. (These are *experiences*, not page names — Phase 3 validates their structure.)

| # | Chapter | Its role in the story | Why it follows the previous one | Emotional job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Welcome** | "This is EVOKE. Bring out the best in You." | — (the arrival beat) | Calm, oriented, invited |
| 2 | **What EVOKE is** | The festival in one honest breath: an inter-school festival of sixteen events; four worlds of student talent | Welcome's promise needs a face | Curious, safe |
| 3 | **The pulse** | A glimpse of transformation — the before/after that is the brand's DNA (Phase 1 §1.1) | "What do you mean?" needs proof | Inspired |
| 4 | **The stages** | The sixteen events in four natures: Stage, Mind, Art, Maker (Phase 0 §4.17) | "What can I do?" needs a landscape | Excited, choosing |
| 5 | **One stage** | The chosen event in full: personality, rules, teams, materials, time, judging (Phase 0 §6.5 mini-experience) | "That one." needs "what exactly?" | Focused, certain |
| 6 | **The decision** | Choice restated; what registration needs (TBD); the act of committing | Certainty needs a doorway | Confident, committed |
| 7 | **The confirmation** | "You're registered." What happens next; when updates arrive | Commitment needs a receipt | Triumphant, safe |
| 8 | **Your space** | The registered participant's return home: status, updates, announcements (Phase 0 12.5) | "What now?" needs a place | Anticipatory, secure |
| 9 | **The quiet corners** | About the host college (Q19 pending), contact (Q12 pending), how rules are decided, FAQ | Doubts need answers somewhere | Reassured |

### 3.2 The sequence logic

- **Curiosity rises across chapters 1→4:** each chapter answers one question and opens the next (Part 5).
- **Certainty rises across chapters 5→7:** rules → choice → confirmation (Charter §5).
- **Excitement is highest at chapter 4** and deliberately cools at 5 so the mind can work (Phase 1.5 Part 11.3: quiet after dense).
- **Calm returns at chapter 9** — the end of the film, where the world is quiet and trustworthy (Phase 1 §2.3 logistics calm; Bible 13.6).
- **Chapters are not equal in size or time:** 4 and 5 carry the most content; 1, 2, 6, 7 carry very little (Phase 1.5 Part 11.3 density is content-typed).

### 3.3 The chapter that is not a chapter

There is no "all sixteen events at full detail, in parallel" moment before the participant has chosen a world. The landscape is grouped by nature (Stage, Mind, Art, Maker) so choosing feels like *finding*, not browsing a catalogue (Phase 0 §4.17; Phase 0 12.4 no repeated layouts; Bible 6.5 one grammar, sixteen dialects).

**Trace:** Phase 0 §6.2, §6.5, §12.4/12.10; Phase 1 §2.1; Phase 1.5 Part 4, Part 11.3; Charter §3.

## PART 4 — The Scroll Storyboard

### 4.1 The directed scroll (the first-visit descent)

The first visit is one directed descent — a story told in beats over time, not a series of sections. Each beat gives the visitor one thing to feel and one reason to continue. (This describes what is discovered over time; it says nothing about placement.)

| Time (approx.) | Beat | The visitor discovers | Why they continue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 s | The promise | "Bring out the best in You." | A tagline that addresses *them* (Phase 1 §2.1 Peak 1) |
| ~5 s | The identity | "An inter-school festival of sixteen events, for students like you." | Now they know it is for them |
| ~10 s | The pulse | A transformation moment — something ordinary becoming extraordinary | They want to feel that again |
| ~15 s | The four worlds | Stage · Mind · Art · Maker — the natures of the sixteen events | They are finding their world |
| ~25 s | The landscape | Sixteen events, grouped by world, each with a personality | "Which one is mine?" becomes a game |
| ~35 s | The glimpse | One event's rule in plain words — "team of two, ten slips, eight correct" | Rules this honest feel safe |
| ~45 s | The call | "Your stage is waiting." | The story turns to *them* |
| ~55 s | The closing | "See you there." — the festival hands back to the visitor | The story is complete; the visitor decides |

### 4.2 Rules of the scroll

1. **Directed, never endless:** the descent has a beginning (the promise) and an end (the closing). After the closing, the visitor has left the film and entered the practical space of the platform. (Bible 13.3: content creates hierarchy; Phase 0 12.10: one continuous narrative.)
2. **One discovery at a time:** no beat asks for two discoveries simultaneously (Phase 1.5 Part 4.3).
3. **The hush is a beat:** the spaces between beats are where the visitor processes and the next question forms (Phase 1.5 Part 4.1: sections end with a settlement; Part 11.3: the hush is a tempo).
4. **Why they continue:** each beat answers the question the previous beat raised — and ends on a new question. When the beats stop raising questions, the descent ends.
5. **Skimmers are honored:** the visitor may stop the descent at any beat and switch to exploring; the story never withholds information behind the scroll (Part 9, Information Release Strategy — timing, not hiding).
6. **The scroll never performs tricks** (blacklist 41, 40; Phase 1.5 Part 7.1.6): the natural scroll is plain; what is staged is *what appears when*, never how the scrollbar behaves.

**Trace:** Phase 0 §8 scroll-depth metric, §12.10; Phase 1 §2.1–2.2, §9; Phase 1.5 Part 4, Part 7, Part 11.3.

## PART 5 — The Curiosity Curve

### 5.1 The curve

```
Curiosity
  high ──╮                     ╭── landscape (peak)
         │                    ╱ ╲
  med ────╲──────────────────╱───╲────────────╱── conversion (curiosity
          ╲                ╱      ╲          ╱      hands over to certainty)
  low ─────╲──────────────╱        ╲────────╱─────── commitment
            ╲────────────╱  rules    ╱───────╱─ anticipation (post-registration)
             arrival  identity  pulse   worlds  landscape  rules  decision  post-reg
  time:      0s       5s       10s    15s    25s      35s    45s      55s+
```

### 5.2 The key points

| Point | Curiosity level | The question driving it |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival (0–3 s) | Medium, cautious | "What is this, and is it for me?" |
| Identity (~5 s) | Rising | "What actually happens there?" |
| Pulse (~10 s) | Rising | "Could that be me?" |
| Four worlds (~15 s) | High | "Which world is mine?" |
| Landscape (~25 s) | **Peak** | "Which event is mine?" |
| Rules (~35 s) | Plateau → converting | "Do I qualify? Can I really do this?" |
| Decision (~45 s) | Curiosity hands over to certainty | "Should I commit?" |
| Confirmation (55 s+) | A new species: anticipation | "What happens next?" |

### 5.3 Why the curve is shaped this way

- **Curiosity needs a question:** each answer raises the next question, keeping the curve climbing until the participant has a target — an event (Phase 1 §1.1: "hands everyone a slip"; Phase 0 §1.5 curiosity).
- **The peak belongs to choice:** at "which event is mine?" curiosity is highest — this is the festival's true promise (Phase 0 §4.17; Phase 1 §2.1: 10–30 s self-placement).
- **The conversion point is the rules:** curiosity cannot survive contact with real commitment without becoming certainty. If the platform keeps selling energy past this point, it breaks Progressive Confidence (Charter §5). If it goes flat too early, it breaks the promise (Phase 1.5 EVOKE Test #6).
- **After commitment, curiosity changes species:** it becomes anticipation of participation — from then on the platform's job is feeding that anticipation with truthful updates (Phase 0 12.5: announcements as source of truth; Phase 0 1.5 anticipation/excitement).

**Trace:** Phase 0 §1.5, §12.10; Phase 1 §2.1–2.4; Phase 1.5 Part 9; Charter §5.

## PART 6 — Emotional Temperature

### 6.1 The temperature map

| Stage of journey | Dominant emotion | Why this transition happens |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival | **Calm** | A stranger needs safety before anything else; calm is the price of entry (Phase 1 §2.3) |
| Identity beat | **Curious** | "This is for students like me" opens the first door |
| Pulse | **Inspired** | Transformation is the brand's DNA (Phase 1 §1.1) — inspiration is its proof |
| Landscape | **Excited** | Choice among sixteen personal stages; belonging: "one of these is mine" |
| Rules | **Focused** | Energy deliberately cools (Phase 1.5 11.3: quiet after dense) so the mind can work |
| Decision | **Confident** | Every question answered before the doorway (Charter §5) |
| Confirmation | **Committed + triumphant** | The receipt moment — "I'm in" |
| Your space | **Anticipatory, secure** | The future is visible and the participant holds it |
| Quiet corners | **Reassured** | Doubts have somewhere honest to be answered |

### 6.2 Transitions that must never happen

- **Excited → rushed** (payment pressure): urgency is a trust-destroyer at decision time (Part 15.4); deadline honesty comes from the organizer's real dates, never from manufactured countdowns (blacklist 13).
- **Focused → overwhelmed:** the rules must not multiply questions; a rule that raises a question is answered in place (Part 10).
- **Confident → confused:** nothing after the decision may contradict what came before (Part 10.2: no surprises by design).
- **Calm → bored:** calm is a beat, not a state; the rhythm alternates (Phase 1.5 11.3).

### 6.3 The temperature rule

Only one dominant temperature per moment. The platform moves between temperatures through the beats, and the hush between beats is where the temperature changes (Phase 1.5 Part 4.1: a section ends with a settlement; Part 4.7: transitions are gestures). *(Phase 1 §1.2 energy profile: energy that breathes — peak, then cast.)*

**Trace:** Phase 0 §1.5, §2.3; Phase 1 §1.2, §2.1–2.3; Phase 1.5 Part 4, Part 11.3; Charter §5.

## PART 7 — Cognitive Load Map

### 7.1 The mental rhythm (think → relax → think → relax)

| Zone | Load | What the visitor's mind is asked to do |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival | Very low | Nothing but "who is this for" |
| Identity | Low | Accept one statement |
| Pulse | Low | Feel, don't think |
| Landscape | Medium | Choose a world, then scan events |
| One stage — the event | **High** | Compare self against rules: eligibility, team, materials, time |
| Decision | Low → Medium | Confirm choice; provide only what registration needs (TBD) |
| Confirmation | Very low | Receive the receipt and the next step |
| Your space | Medium (on return) | Check status, read updates |
| Quiet corners | Low | Find one answer |

### 7.2 Where the visitor should think — and where not

- **Think:** choosing among events; checking their own fit against the rules; deciding to commit. These are the platform's purpose (Charter §3).
- **Relax:** arrival, pulse, transitions, confirmation. No thinking is demanded at emotional moments (Phase 1 §2.2: Peak 1 → Valley 1).
- **Become dense:** the event's rules — as scannable, structured facts, never as prose (Phase 0 12.6; Phase 1.5 11.3: density is content-typed).
- **Disappear:** during commitment, everything except the task at hand recedes — the rules were answered earlier (Part 10); after confirmation, everything except the next step recedes (Phase 1.5 11.3: open → impact → silence).

### 7.3 Rules of the load map

1. **Never overwhelm:** no moment asks the visitor to hold more than one new idea — one event at a time, one rule family at a time, one decision at a time (Phase 1.5 Part 4.3; Phase 0 5.3 progressive disclosure).
2. **Never under-inform:** the event moment is where completeness is mandatory — a missing rule is a question that travels to the form (Part 10) and becomes abandonment (Phase 0 2.1 frustrations: "being disqualified over a small detail").
3. **Load alternates per moment, not per journey:** the journey alternates light and heavy beats so that the heavy moments have the visitor's full mind (Phase 1 §1.2: energy that breathes).

**Trace:** Phase 0 §2.1–2.2, §5.3, §12.6; Phase 1 §2.3; Phase 1.5 Part 4, Part 11.3.

## PART 8 — Attention Architecture

### 8.1 One primary message per moment

The visitor's attention is a single spotlight (Phase 1.5: the platform is built on light). At every moment exactly one thing is primary:

| Moment | Primary | Everything else (secondary, quiet) |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival | The promise | Identity details, dates (TBD), entry points |
| Identity | What EVOKE is | Logistics |
| Pulse | The transformation | The voice that frames it |
| Landscape | The choice: which world / which event | Extras, secondary facts |
| One stage | The rules that decide "can I" | The surrounding personality |
| Decision | The commitment step | Everything already answered |
| Confirmation | The receipt + next step | Everything else |
| Your space | What changed since last visit | All that hasn't |

### 8.2 What must never compete

- The primary message of a moment never competes with another message (Phase 1.5 Part 4.3: one event at a time; Bible 13.3: content creates hierarchy).
- Announcement-style energy (countdowns, deadline urgency) never appears during thinking moments — urgency at decision time destroys trust (Part 15.4; blacklist 13).
- The promise never fights the practical: when the visitor is working (rules, forms), the festival's energy stands back and lets the facts speak (Phase 1 §2.3; Bible 13.5: the loud is rationed).

### 8.3 How attention travels

- **First visit:** the platform *leads* attention — the descent (Part 4); attention follows the questions (Phase 0 12.10: emotion first, information second, action third).
- **Choosing:** attention is *handed* to the visitor — the landscape is for scanning; nothing grabs (Part 3.3: finding, not catalogue).
- **Working:** attention is *focused* by structure — the rules moment walks the eye along a short, known path (scannable facts, one rule family at a time — Part 7).
- **Returning:** attention is *greeted* by change — what is new since the last visit comes first (Phase 0 12.5: announcements as source of truth).

**Trace:** Phase 0 §12.10/12.12, §6.2; Phase 1 §2.1–2.3; Phase 1.5 Part 4, Part 11.3.

## PART 9 — Information Release Strategy

### 9.0 When, not where

Information is organized by **timing**. The platform releases each fact exactly when the visitor's mind is ready for it — before it is asked, and never earlier than it matters. (This is the object Phase 3 must translate into structure and content inventory.)

### 9.1 The release timeline

| Information | Released | Why then |
|---|---|---|
| What EVOKE is | Arrival (instant) | Orientation is the first need |
| Who it is for | Arrival | Belonging is the first question |
| The tagline / promise | Arrival | The emotional frame |
| The sixteen events exist, in four worlds | First 20–30 s | The landscape, not the details |
| One world's personality | On choosing a world | Relevance before detail |
| One event's personality | On choosing an event | "Is this me?" |
| **Rules: eligibility, teams, materials, time, judging** | **At the event, in full, before any commitment** | Confidence requires completeness (Charter §5) |
| Registration requirements (TBD) | At the decision moment, before the form | Nothing asked may be a surprise |
| Payment facts (TBD) | At the decision moment | Trust at the money moment |
| Confirmation + next steps | Immediately after registering | The receipt closes the loop |
| Status, updates, announcements | In the participant's space, ongoing | The return engine (Phase 0 12.5) |
| Contact, FAQ, host details (TBD) | In the quiet corners, always findable | Doubt needs a home |

### 9.2 Release rules

1. **Never release a fact before its moment:** an early dump of all sixteen events' rules overwhelms (Part 7.3; Phase 0 5.3 progressive disclosure).
2. **Never withhold a fact past its moment:** rules are never revealed at the form; payment terms (TBD) are never revealed at the payment step (Part 10.2: no surprises by design).
3. **Placeholder honesty:** where official details do not exist yet (dates, fees, registration mechanics), the platform says so plainly — an honest "announced soon" preserves trust; silence destroys it (Charter §7; Phase 0 12.5; blacklist 5 — no invented placeholder content).
4. **The moment a fact becomes relevant is the moment it is released — that is the entire strategy.**

**Trace:** Phase 0 §5.3, §6.2, §10, §12.5; Phase 1.5 Part 11.3; Charter §5, §7.

## PART 10 — Registration Psychology

### 10.1 The questions that exist before registration

| Question | Where it is answered (before the doorway) |
|---|---|
| Can I participate? | Identity beat + eligibility at the event (TBD) |
| Am I eligible — class, age, school? | The event's facts (TBD from organizers — Phase 0 Q5) |
| What if I don't have teammates? | The event's team rule + what solo/small-team participants do (Phase 0 Q10, Q9 — TBD) |
| How long does it last? | The event's facts (time limits from the rule book — Phase 0 §3.1) |
| What should I bring? | The event's materials facts (off-stage: own materials — Phase 0 3.3.2) |
| How many members / what's the max? | The event's team facts |
| What happens after registration? | The confirmation moment — next steps, then the participant's space |
| What if it's full / closed? | Failure journeys (Part 14) — honest, with alternatives |
| Payment questions | **TBD** — never invented; when official, released at the decision moment (Part 9) |

### 10.2 The principle: certainty before the doorway

- The moment of commitment **restates** what the visitor is about to do — "You're registering for X, team of two, with Y" — so the decision is confirmed before any form begins. **No question is answered by a form; the form only collects what was already announced** (Charter §5: "I know what information I need").
- If a rule would change the decision, it was already released at the event (Part 9); the decision moment contains **no surprises by design** (Phase 0 2.1: frustration = "being disqualified over a small detail").
- The visitor's last thought before committing is "yes" — never "wait —". If any "wait —" is possible, the earlier release failed.

### 10.3 The psychological contract of registration

1. The platform states plainly who registers — school or individual (TBD, Phase 0 Q4).
2. It states what will be asked, before asking it.
3. It states what happens after — confirmation, next steps, updates.
4. It never invents payment or fee facts (Charter §7; Phase 0 Q8).

### 10.4 Recovery during registration

- Every error the visitor can make is answered in place, in the Ringmaster's honest voice (Phase 1 §1.6 tone table; Bible 11 input personality) — never with jargon, never with blame (Part 14).
- Leaving mid-registration is treated as "returning later", not "failed" (Part 14; Part 13.3 session continuity).
- The confirmation is the receipt: what was registered, for whom, and what happens next (Part 12.2).

**Trace:** Phase 0 §2.1–2.2, §7.4, §10; Phase 1 §1.6, §11; Charter §3, §5, §7.

### 11.1 The main path

```
Arrival
  │
  ▼
Understand EVOKE (identity beat)
  │
  ▼
Choose a world → choose an event
  │
  ├── Need more information? ──► read the event's full rules ──► return to the event
  │
  ▼
Can I participate? (eligibility check)
  │
  ├── No ──► honest answer + alternatives (another event / contact / when to ask — TBD)
  │
  ▼
Still interested? (the decision moment)
  │
  ├── Yes
  ▼
Register (flow TBD) ──► Confirmation ──► Your space
  │
  └── No ──► leave informed: bookmarked, shared, or with a known reason to return
```

### 11.2 Alternative paths (all intentional)

- **Deep entry** (QR on a poster for one event): enters at the event — the identity beat compresses to one line, the event is immediate (Part 12.1).
- **Returning participant:** enters at "Your space" — what changed comes first (Part 8.3).
- **Coordinator / school staff** (Phase 0 §2.2): a different need — information and control, not discovery; served without making the participant journey bureaucratic (Phase 0 12.1: never a dashboard feel).
- **Shared link:** lands mid-journey — always re-anchored: "you're here because someone shared this" (Part 12.1).

### 11.3 Recovery paths

- Lost mid-scroll or mid-flow: returning is always possible; nothing that was already done is lost (state retention — TBD mechanics, fixed contract).
- Doubt at the doorway: the quiet corners (FAQ, contact — TBD) are one step away, never a search expedition.
- Failure mid-registration: the Part 14 paths; every failure ends with a next step.

### 11.4 Drop-off risks (honest list)

| Risk point | Why | Mitigation (experience-level) |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival | Indistinguishable from "another school site" | The promise beat addresses the visitor directly (Phase 1.5 EVOKE Test #1, #3) |
| Landscape | Choice paralysis at sixteen events | Four worlds narrow choice before details appear (Part 3.3) |
| Rules density | Rules read like homework | Structured, scannable facts; one rule family at a time (Part 7) |
| Rule unanswered | "How many members?" missing | Completeness is mandatory at the event (Part 9) |
| The doorway | Surprise requirements | Nothing asked that was not announced (Part 10) |
| Payment (TBD) | Opaque or urgent | Honest, unhurried, fully explained — by design, not by luck |
| After confirmation | Silence | The next step is stated; the participant's space exists (Part 14.2) |

**Trace:** Phase 0 §7.1–7.5, §8, §12.1; Phase 1.5 Part 9; Charter §3, §6.

## PART 12 — Entry & Exit Strategy

### 12.1 Entries — each one intentional

| Entry | The visitor's state | What the platform owes them first |
|---|---|---|
| Google search ("evoke 2k26", "evoke events") | Task-minded, knows the name | Orientation in one beat; the promise |
| QR code from a poster | Excited, pointed at one event | The event, immediately; identity in a line (Phase 0 §7.2: QR from school notice) |
| Direct link (WhatsApp, school) | Trusted already, possibly mid-journey | Re-anchoring: where they are in the story |
| Social media | Casual, curious | The promise + shareability; the story's first beats |
| College / school site | Institutional | Credibility + the official identity (host, contact — TBD, Q19/Q12) |

**Rule:** every entry is recognized as a different emotional state. The opening beat adapts to what the visitor arrived *for* — the platform never assumes everyone arrived at the beginning of the story (Part 11.2 deep entry; Phase 0 5.2.7 audience-aware paths).

### 12.2 Exits — every exit has purpose

| Exit | The visitor's state | The platform's gift on the way out |
|---|---|---|
| Registered | Committed | Confirmation + what happens next (Part 10) |
| Returning later | Not ready yet | A known place to return to; nothing lost (Part 13.3) |
| Bookmarking | Interested | The story remembered by its name — the bookmark holds |
| Sharing | Enthusiastic | The share carries a moment worth sharing (the pulse, a single event) |
| Returning to their space | Registered again | What changed since last visit (Part 8.3) |
| Leaving uninformed | — | **Must not exist:** no exit ends in silence; every path leaves a question answered or a way back |

**Rule:** the platform never holds the visitor hostage — but it never lets them leave confused. Silence on exit is the only failure (Part 15.4: what destroys trust). *(Phase 0 1.7: the next step — no ambiguity left behind; Phase 0 §8: return visits metric.)*

## PART 13 — Mobile-First Experience

### 13.1 The psychology of the phone

- Sessions are **short and interruptible** — a glance during recess, at home, on the bus. Every moment must be completable or resumable (Phase 0 11.4: students browse on phones).
- Reading is **slow and costly** — sentences shrink, facts chunk (Part 7), one idea per beat (Part 4).
- The thumb is **lazy and honest** — the primary action of any moment must be reachable without thinking about reach; attention follows the thumb's natural path (experience principle — structure for Phase 4).
- Scrolling is **native; tapping is a commitment** — the story is told by scroll (Part 4); decisions are made by deliberate tap. No accidental commitments; every commitment is confirmable (Part 10).
- There is **no hover** — curiosity is served by making more available, not by anticipation tricks (Phase 1 §11 hover philosophy; blacklist 42).
- Context **switches constantly** (WhatsApp → platform → back) — each return re-orients in one beat (Part 12.1 re-anchoring).

### 13.2 What changes for mobile

| Desktop-era default | The mobile experience |
|---|---|
| Parallel information (side-by-side) | One thing at a time — the story beats, not a grid (Phase 1.5 Part 4.2) |
| Comparison shopping | Guided choice: four worlds → one event (Part 3.3) |
| Long forms (TBD) | Registration asks only what was announced, in the announced order (Part 10) |
| Hover-revealed details | Details live in the open, where a tap cannot miss them |
| Permanent visible navigation | Navigation is a quiet escape hatch, never a competing voice (Part 8.2) |

### 13.3 The mobile golden rules

1. Every moment is **finishable in one hand, in under a minute of thought** — except the event's rules, which are complete but chunked (Part 7).
2. Every interrupted journey **resumes without penalty** (TBD mechanics; the experience contract is fixed — Part 11.3).
3. The phone visitor **never feels small** — the festival comes to their hand (Phase 1 §1.1: the Ringmaster hands everyone a slip; Bible 13.20: mobile is not a smaller desktop).

**Trace:** Phase 0 §2.1, §8 mobile metrics, §11.4, §12.9; Phase 1 §5.1, §11; Phase 1.5 Part 4.

## PART 14 — Failure Journeys

### 14.1 The law of failure

When something fails, the visitor must feel: **calm (not blamed), informed (not confused), and hopeful (not stuck)**. Every failure ends with a next step. **No dead ends exist in the platform** (blacklist 47; Phase 0 12.2: animation/purpose and honesty culture).

### 14.2 The journeys

| Failure | How the visitor feels (target) | What happens next |
|---|---|---|
| Registration closed | Calm, informed, included for next time | The platform says when/why honestly (TBD), keeps the participant informed (announcement path); no urgency games (blacklist 13) |
| Event full | Slight disappointment, not rejection | The event says it plainly; the participant is offered the nearest alternative and the informed/waiting path (TBD) |
| Network failure | Unbothered | The moment resumes where it stopped (Part 13.3); nothing is lost; retry is one action |
| Payment pending (TBD) | Secure, not anxious | The pending state is visible and explained in the participant's space; the next step is stated |
| Payment failed (TBD) | Calm, unhurried | The failure is named plainly; retry is one action; entered data is not lost; a contact path exists |
| Session expired | Unfazed | Return is one action; what was done is still done |
| Wrong credentials (TBD) | Guided, not blocked | The recovery path is stated in place (reset / contact — TBD) |
| No teammate | Included, not stuck | The event's team rule + the honest options: solo path, ask-a-friend guidance (TBD) |
| No search result | Understood | The platform says what it does and does not contain (sixteen events, four worlds) and offers the path to the landscape |

### 14.3 The voice of failure

Failure moments speak in the Ringmaster's honest voice (Phase 1 §1.6): warm, precise; never joking about the visitor's trouble, never robotic. "That stage is full — but this one is still open" is the tone; "Error 403" is forbidden (Bible 11: errors shown with text, never color alone — blacklist 51).

### 14.4 Trust under failure

Failure is where trust is made or broken. A platform that handles failure calmly is **more trusted after the failure than before it** (Part 15; Phase 0 2.3 parents: transparency builds buy-in).

**Trace:** Phase 0 §2.1, §2.3, §9; Phase 1 §1.6, §11; Phase 1.5 Part 6.4 (Stamp), Part 11.1 (named exceptions); Charter §7.

## PART 15 — Trust Architecture

### 15.1 What builds trust

- **Honesty of facts** — rules are exact and complete; nothing invented (Phase 0 §10 [MISSING]; Charter §7).
- **Placeholder honesty** — what is not yet official is said to be pending: "announced soon" beats silence (Part 9.2).
- **Official presence** — the host college and a real contact path (Q12, Q19 — TBD) are visible in the quiet corners (Phase 0 §2.3 parents: reassurance).
- **The rule book's own authority** — "Judges' decision is final" is presented as the festival's promise of fairness, not a threat (Phase 0 3.3.1; Phase 1.5 Part 6.4: the Stamp).
- **Progress feedback** — every action the visitor takes leaves a visible, truthful mark: confirmation, status, updates (Part 10.4).
- **Consistency of voice and world** — the festival never switches tone between selling and serving (Phase 1 §1.6; Part 6.2).
- **Restraint** — urgency, countdown pressure, and "limited seats" never appear where honesty would suffer (Part 8.2; blacklist 13).

### 15.2 When reassurance appears

- At the doorway (Part 10): a restatement of choice — "here is exactly what you're registering for."
- At the money moment (TBD): complete, unhurried payment facts, released at the moment (Part 9).
- After the receipt: what happens next, when updates come, where to return (Part 12.2).
- At every quiet corner: contact (TBD) and how rules are decided.

### 15.3 How uncertainty is reduced

1. By answering questions **before** they are asked at the doorway (Part 10).
2. By never letting a fact arrive late (Part 9.2).
3. By treating pending information honestly (Part 15.1).

### 15.4 What destroys trust (forbidden)

- Hidden fees or late payment facts (TBD — must never appear as surprises).
- Invented rules, invented dates, invented eligibility (Phase 0 12.5: no placeholder content).
- Urgency where there is no real deadline (the deadline is real only when the organizer says so — Phase 0 Q1).
- Vague promises without substance.
- Dead ends, silent failures, unreachable contact (Part 14.1).
- A change of voice between selling and serving (Phase 1 §1.6).

**Trace:** Phase 0 §1.5, §2.2–2.3, §3.3, §9, §12.5; Phase 1 §1.6, §2.3; Phase 1.5 Part 9; Charter §5–§7.

| Category | Success criteria |
|---|---|
| **Psychological** | First-time visitors can answer "what is EVOKE?" and "could I participate?" after the first beats; the five Progressive Confidence statements (Charter §5) hold at each stage; no moment raises an unanswered question |

## PART 17 — Self Critique

### 17.1 Where Experience Direction could fail

1. **Story over function.** The five-act frame could tempt later phases into long cinematic entrances that delay the task. Mitigation: the arrival is one beat; the story serves the journey (Charter §2).
2. **Misread as layout.** "Beats," "descent," and "attention" could be read as prescriptions for hero sections and scroll mechanics. Mitigation: Phase 3 must translate timing into structure and Phase 4 into wireframes — this document defines *what*, never *how it looks*.
3. **Generic flows in disguise.** If chapters become conventional nav labels (Home / About / Events / Contact), the EVOKE Test fails (Phase 1.5 Part 9 #1, #2). Mitigation: the chapter map is a story sequence, not a menu (Part 3.3).
4. **Density miscalculation.** The rules moment demands completeness — but completeness written as prose becomes a wall. Mitigation: Phase 3/4 must structure rules as scannable facts; this document fixes the *timing*, not the format.
5. **Assumptions still made.** Dates, venue, registration model, school vs individual, payment, admin model, device mix, and the official event list remain TBD. Every assumption above is flagged and traces to Phase 0 questions (Q1–Q5, Q12, Q19).
6. **The nine-step journey may compress.** Real content may fold beats together. Mitigation: beats are emotional, not physical — they may share a moment but never a question.
7. **"When not where" ambiguity.** Release timing without structure may confuse implementers. Mitigation: Phase 3 (Information Architecture Validation) exists precisely to translate this.
8. **Placeholder drift.** TBD gaps invite designers to invent flows. Mitigation: an explicit gate — no flow is designed until its official facts exist (Charter §7; Part 10.3).

### 17.2 Where developers could misunderstand

- **"Progressive Confidence" is not a feature**; it is a property of the whole journey — it cannot be built into one screen (Charter §5).
- **"No dead ends" is a contract across the platform**, including future failure states (Part 14).
- **The failure voice (14.3) is tone-of-product**, which belongs to content, not styling (Phase 1 §1.6).

### 17.3 What Phase 3 must inherit

- The chapter map as the object of Information Architecture validation (Part 3).
- The release timeline as the object of content/IA validation (Part 9).
- The entry states (Part 12) as the object of entry-point design.
- The participant's space (Parts 11, 14, 15) as a first-class citizen, not an afterthought.

**Trace:** Phase 0 §9, §10, §15; Phase 1 §15; Phase 1.5 Part 8, Part 9; Charter §7.

## PART 18 — Memory Architecture (addendum from the Phase 2 review)

*What the participant carries with them after they leave — and how the platform decides what that is.*

### 18.1 The memory question

If the visitor remembers nothing, the experience failed. Memory is designed in time horizons:

| After | The participant should still hold | How it is proven |
|---|---|---|
| 30 seconds | The sentence — "Bring out the best in You." The feeling: "I could be that, on a stage." One event that belongs to them | They can say what it was, in seconds (Phase 1 §2.4; Phase 0 1.7) |
| 5 hours | A story worth telling — a surprising rule ("team of two, ten slips, eight correct"), the transformation they saw, their own confirmation | They can tell a friend in one sentence (Phase 0 §8 share action) |
| Next week | Their event, the date (TBD), and the knowledge that their place is held and updates will come | They return or stay awaiting announcements (Phase 0 12.5 return engine) |

### 18.2 Memory Anchors (moments designed to stick)

Not content — *moments*. The platform owns a small, fixed set, each repeated and reused instead of improvised:

1. **The Promise** — the tagline in its signature setting (Bible 5.2); the opening beat (Part 4, 0–3 s).
2. **The Transformation** — the before/after witness (Phase 1 §1.1): a carved vegetable, a painted pot, waste-to-wonder; the proof beat (Part 4, ~10 s).
3. **The Surprising Rule** — a rule so exact it becomes a story ("ten slips, eight correct to win"); honest facts are memorable stories (Phase 0 §3.3).
4. **The Calling Beat** — "Your stage is waiting." — the moment the story turns to the visitor (Part 4, ~45 s).
5. **The Stamp** — the confirmation receipt (Phase 1.5 Part 6.4 Stamp); the "I'm registered" verdict.

### 18.3 Rules of anchors

1. **The share-variable law:** if an anchor cannot be repeated to a friend in one sentence, it is not an anchor.
2. **Five anchors, no more** — the loud is rationed (Bible 13.5).
3. **They all rhyme:** each anchor is an act of revealing (Part 3), so the five reinforce one identity and one memory (Phase 1.5 Part 3.3: nothing appears without being unveiled).

**Trace:** Phase 0 §1.7, §8; Phase 1 §1.1, §2.4, §5.2; Phase 1.5 Part 3, Part 6.4; Charter §2.

## PART 19 — Anticipation Architecture (addendum from the Phase 2 review)

### 19.1 The second arc

The participant journey does **not end at registration**. It continues: *Register → Confirm → Anticipate → Arrive → Experience → Remember.* Registration is the hinge of the story, not its conclusion — participants should be **more** excited after registering than before the decision.

### 19.2 The anticipation arc (after the doorway)

| Stage | What the platform provides | Emotional job |
|---|---|---|
| Immediately after confirmation | The receipt + the stated next step | Triumph turns into the first true anticipation |
| The waiting window | The participant's space, the official countdown when the date exists, updates when they exist (TBD — placeholder food) | The festival clock (Phase 1.5 Part 6.6) finds its true home |
| Approach (near the date) | Their event's exact requirements: what to bring, phase of preparation | Knowledge grows; so does quiet confidence |
| Event day | When, where, their slot, what to do | Calm and ready, not panic |
| After | The verdict (the Stamp), the remembered good moments, the next edition | The final, shared beat (Phase 0 §5.5 lifecycle) |

### 19.3 The rules of anticipation

1. **Anticipation is truthful, never engineered** — a pending date says "announced soon" (Charter §7); no fabricated surprises (Part 14.3; blacklist 13).
2. **Your Space is the heartbeat of anticipation** — the same home the platform gave the registered participant (Part 8; Phase 0 12.5) is also its anticipation engine.
3. **The stamped receipt is protected** — after confirmation the platform never asks, never sells, never pressures; the next step simply stands at the door (Part 12.2; Part 23 silence after confirmation).

**Trace:** Phase 0 §5.5, §6.4, §12.5; Phase 1.5 Part 6.6, Part 11.1 exceptions; Charter §3, §5.

## PART 20 — Recovery Experience

### 20.1 Recovery is not "error handling"

An error page reports the failure; a **recovery experience rebuilds confidence**. The professional question is not "what went wrong?" but **"how quickly can we rebuild the visitor's confidence?"** (Part 14 mapped how each failure should feel; this part defines the rebuild).

### 20.2 The recovery contract (four steps, always in order)

1. **Say it plainly** — the failure in the Ringmaster's honest voice; no jargon, no blame (Part 14.3; Phase 1 §1.6 Rule Keeper).
2. **Show what is preserved** — nothing already entered was lost; state persists (Part 13.3).
3. **Offer exactly one path** — a single clear way back to the journey (Part 14.2).
4. **Let silence settle** — one beat of calm before anything else; never rush a recovered visitor (Part 14.1, Part 23).

### 20.3 Recovery by kind

| Kind | The rebuild |
|---|---|
| **Data** (missed entry, wrong input, wrong credentials — TBD) | Fixed in place; nothing resets; the platform visibly keeps their effort |
| **Money** (payment failed / pending — TBD) | State visible, retry one step, "no double-charge" stated plainly, contact path — the money trust-killer (Part 15.4) |
| **Eligibility** (not eligible) | An honest "no" + the nearest alternative + a real path to ask (when official rules arrive — TBD) |
| **Availability** (event full) | The event states it plainly; the participant is offered the nearest alternative and the informed path (Part 14.2) |
| **Context** (session, network) | Resume exactly where they stopped; an interrupted visit is never a lost visit (Part 11.3) |
| **Confidence (the meta-recovery)** | After any failure the *next step itself* restores: recovery is where the platform proves it runs the world with the participant, not against them |

### 20.4 The law

A participant who failed and was recovered **trusts the platform more than one who never failed** (Part 14.4). In what follows, recovery is a designed feature and part of how the platform is experienced, not an exception to be handled.

## PART 21 — Time Architecture

The experience is a function of **time**, not just of place. The platform always lives in a season (Phase 0 §5.5 lifecycle; §6.4 content states), and its behavior in each season is intentional.

### 21.1 The seasons

| Season | What the visitor is doing | What the platform leads with | What must never be stale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Announcement** | Curious, not committed | The promise; what EVOKE is; the four worlds | A fake deadline — the schedule and dates are real only when announced (Part 22.2, TBD) |
| **Registration window** | Deciding → committing | The doorway; the honest deadline (TBD); the journey beats (Parts 2–5) | Any ambiguous "register" state |
| **Approach** (after registration, before the date) | Ready, registered | Their event and their space; dates and requirements as announced (TBD) | Any trace of "are you sure you want to?" — they earned the door already (Part 19) |
| **Event day** | There | What to know, where to go, their slot (TBD) | All "register" language |
| **Post-event** | Remembering | Winners (Stamp), the gallery, the next edition | Anything claiming the event hasn't happened |

### 21.2 Rules of time

1. **The platform always knows which season it is** and presents only that season's reality (Phase 0 5.2.8: stale registration pages are the #1 festival-site failure).
2. **Every content unit carries its season** — dates "announced soon" until the real one exists (Charter §7, Part 9.2).
3. **Different visitors live in different season shapes at once** — a first-time visitor watches the announcement, a registered participant watches the approach, and a fan watches the recap; the platform is a single platform rendered per visitor's own season (Part 12.7).

## PART 22 — Human Energy (a second emotional lens)

Parts 6 and 7 mapped how the participant *feels* and how hard the mind *works*. Energy adds a tension axis: **how much effort the moment demands.**

### 22.1 The energy curve

```
Energy
high ──╮                             confirmation ──╮
       │   discovery                (the stamp)     │
mid ───────╲        ╲          ╱───────╲───────────╱
            ╲        ╲        ╱ (form)   ╲         energy released
            ╲        ╲      ╱  (low)      ╲       and replenished
low ─────────╲        ╲    ╱               ╲
              ╲        ╲  ╱                 ╲
       arrival  word  event  decision  form  payment  confirm  anticipation
```

### 22.2 The two need the platform

1. **The low-energy form:** form-filling is inherently low-energy and honest — the platform controls it rather than trying to "energize" it (that would be friction). What it does instead: never ask twice (Part 11.3), never present a forced turn (Part 7), celebrate the end of the form with the Stamp (which is the *lowest* point, the moment after the last click — the platform gives the peak there, not during).
2. **The relief after the act:** the click's end is a drop in energy; the confirmation Stamp meets that with the highest accepted energy (Part 19.2), turning fall into excitement.

### 22.3 The energy law

- **Do not raise energy where the mind must work** (rules, forms) — raising energy there is friction.
- **Do not drain energy where it is spent** (after last clicks) — reward it.
- **The debit is repaid:** the platform may *borrow* attention and effort (the event page, the form), and it repays attention with the peak moments (the pulse, the stamp) and calm (silence, Part 23). A debt the platform does not repay is a break in Progressive Confidence (Charter §5).

## PART 23 — Silence

*From exhibition design: not every room speaks; some rooms breathe. Where should the participant simply stop and absorb?*

### 23.1 What a silence beat is

A silence beat is a designed pause where **no new information arrives and no action is requested** — the participant's own response becomes the content, and the memory of the moment is given room (Part 18; the hush is a tempo — Phase 1.5 Part 11.3).

### 23.2 The silences of the journey

| Place | What the silence is for |
|---|---|
| After the promise (end of Act I) | The promise is the first anchor; it is allowed to rest before the story moves |
| After the pulse | The transformation is absorbed before the world is introduced — the "wow" gets its moment |
| After the rules (before the doorway) | The decision is made in the quiet — at the moment of the most honest part of the journey (Part 10.2) |
| After the stamp of confirmation | The receipt stays clean, unaddressed — the confirmation plus the one next step, and nothing else (Part 12.2) |
| After a recovery | One beat of calm before the visitor re-enters (Part 14.1, Part 20.2 step 4) |
| After the closing | The story ends in the silence where the visitor chooses to respond |

### 22.3 The rules of silence

1. **Silence has no call to action** — a silence that asks for something is not a silence.
2. **Silence is never a dead end** — the visitor can always move on; the silence is a permission to breathe, not a barrier (Part 12.2).
3. **Silence is composed, not accidental** — it is the designed absence, the same empty space that is the stage floor (Bible 10, Part 4.4): the platform's spacing is its silence, and it is a decision, never a gap.
